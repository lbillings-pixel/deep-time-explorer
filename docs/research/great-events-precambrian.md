Research notes behind the site's texts and pictures, not text written for visitors. Compiled 14 November 2026.

## How to read this dossier

**Citations.** Every reference listed under "Key sources" or "Other sources used" was looked up in the Crossref registry on 14 Nov 2026 (`api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>` or a bibliographic query), and the authors, year, title, journal, volume, pages and DOI given here are as registered there. Exceptions are marked **UNVERIFIED** with the reason. Where Crossref registers only the first page, only the first page is given. Wikipedia was not used as a source.

**Claims.** Each factual statement carries a source key and one of two tags:

- **[abs]** = I read this in the paper's abstract or open full text during this session (via OpenAlex, Europe PMC or Semantic Scholar).
- **[mem]** = the paper exists and is correctly cited, but the specific number or statement is from my recollection of it; the abstract was not retrievable or does not state it. **Treat every [mem] item as needing a check against the paper before use.**

**Painter notes.** Each item is labelled **EVIDENCE** (measured from rocks or samples), **INFERENCE** (model or extrapolation from evidence) or **CONVENTION** (artistic habit with no direct support). Arithmetic that is mine, not a source's, is labelled "my arithmetic".

## 0. Earth forms (text marker only)

**Date.** 4.54 ± 0.05 billion years. The 4.54 Ga figure "in wide use today" comes from Tera's analysis of lead isotopes in four ancient lead deposits (Dalrymple 2001 [abs]); Patterson's first valid age was 4.55 Ga (Dalrymple 2001 [abs]; Patterson 1956, ± 0.07 Ga [mem]). The "± 0.05" (about 1%) is the customary uncertainty attached to Dalrymple's figure [mem]. The Solar System's own "time zero", the oldest solids in meteorites, is 4,567.30 ± 0.16 Ma (Connelly et al. 2012 [abs]); a slightly older 4,568.2 Ma has also been published (Bouvier & Wadhwa 2010 [mem] for the number).

**Key sources**
- Patterson C (1956). Age of meteorites and the earth. *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta* 10(4): 230–237. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(56)90036-9. (Founding paper.)
- Dalrymple GB (2001). The age of the Earth in the twentieth century: a problem (mostly) solved. *Geological Society, London, Special Publications* 190(1): 205–221. doi:10.1144/GSL.SP.2001.190.01.14.
- Connelly JN, Bizzarro M, Krot AN, Nordlund Å, Wielandt D, Ivanova MA (2012). The absolute chronology and thermal processing of solids in the solar protoplanetary disk. *Science* 338(6107): 651–655. doi:10.1126/science.1226919.
- Bouvier A, Wadhwa M (2010). The age of the Solar System redefined by the oldest Pb–Pb age of a meteoritic inclusion. *Nature Geoscience* 3(9): 637–641. doi:10.1038/ngeo941.
- Supporting: Tera F (1981). Aspects of isochronism in Pb isotope systematics—application to planetary evolution. *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta* 45(9): 1439–1448. doi:10.1016/0016-7037(81)90277-5. (Dalrymple's abstract dates this work to 1980; the journal paper registered in Crossref is 1981.)

**What is known**
- Earth's age is not measured on Earth rocks; it comes from lead isotopes in meteorites and in Earth's lead as a whole (Dalrymple 2001 [abs]).
- "Formation" was a process, not a moment: the planet grew over tens of millions of years, and the Moon-forming impact came near the end of that growth (Canup & Asphaug 2001 [abs], see event 1).
- The oldest known Earth material is a zircon grain 4,404 ± 8 Myr old from Jack Hills, Western Australia; the oldest known rock components are 4,030 Myr (Acasta Gneiss) (Wilde et al. 2001 [abs], cited under event 2).

**What is debated.** Whether 4.54 Ga dates accretion, core formation, or the material Earth formed from "is not yet known" (Dalrymple 2001 [abs]). Maurice et al. 2020 [abs] argue the lead age dates the final segregation of the core at ~4.425 Ga.

**For a text marker.** "About 4.54 billion years ago" is safe. Avoid implying an instant.

## 1. Moon-forming giant impact

**Date.** About 4.5 Ga. Recent estimates disagree by about 150 Myr:

| ≥4.51 Ga (lunar crust differentiated by 4.51 Ga; Moon within the first ~60 Myr) | uranium–lead and hafnium in Apollo 14 zircons | Barboni et al. 2017 [abs] |
| 4.47 ± 0.03 Ga (95 ± 32 Myr after condensation); earlier than 40 Myr ruled out at 99.9% | amount of late-accreted material in Earth's mantle plus N-body simulations | Jacobson et al. 2014 [abs] (the Ga conversion is my arithmetic) |

**What is known**
- The Moon is "generally believed to have formed from debris ejected by a large off-centre collision with the early Earth", near the very end of Earth's growth (Canup & Asphaug 2001 [abs]). The giant impact remains "the leading theory" (Lock et al. 2018 [abs]).
- The classic impactor is Mars-sized (Canup & Asphaug 2001, Canup 2004 [mem] for the size; the *Science* editor's summary of the 2012 papers says "a Mars-sized planet" [abs]).
- The impact melted much of Earth's mantle; water and CO2 formed a thick atmosphere, a runaway-greenhouse stage lasted about 10 Myr, and after the water ocean condensed about 100 bar of CO2 remained with the surface near 500 K (Sleep et al. 2014 [abs]).
- The oldest dated lunar zircon is 4,460 ± 31 Ma (Greer et al. 2023 [abs]).

**What is debated**
- The age (table above).
- The kind of impact. The classic model makes the Moon mostly from the impactor, yet Moon and Earth are isotopically alike. Alternatives: two bodies of comparable mass (Canup 2012 [abs, editor's summary]); a fast impact onto a fast-spinning Earth (Ćuk & Stewart 2012 [abs, editor's summary]); a hot doughnut-shaped vapour structure, a "synestia" (Lock et al. 2018 [abs]). These would look different, so any single depiction of the collision is one model among several.

**What a painter could show**
1. *The collision itself*: a Mars-sized body striking obliquely. INFERENCE (simulations); geometry and size are model-dependent, see above. Glowing debris ring afterwards: INFERENCE (Ida et al. 1997; Kokubo et al. 2000, titles verified).
2. *A huge Moon*: simulations assemble the Moon just outside Earth's Roche limit, roughly 3 to 4 Earth radii from Earth's centre, against 60 today (Ida et al. 1997, Kokubo et al. 2000 [mem] for the distance). At ~4 Earth radii the Moon would span roughly 8 to 10 degrees of sky, 15 to 20 times its width today (my arithmetic). INFERENCE. It then retreated; how fast is model-dependent (Zahnle et al. 2015, title verified, details not read).
3. *Earth's surface*: a molten, glowing magma ocean under a dense opaque atmosphere; solid crust only after about 10 Myr; then a hot ocean under ~100 bar of CO2 (Sleep et al. 2014 [abs]). INFERENCE. A scene with a solid, cratered Earth *and* a gigantic Moon combines two different times: CONVENTION.
4. *Day length*: about 5 hours just after the impact (Eulenfeld & Heubeck 2023 [abs]); faster still in the Ćuk & Stewart model. INFERENCE.
5. *Sun*: about 72% as bright as today (see table at top). The sky under a rock-vapour or steam atmosphere would not have been a clear blue; no one can say what colour. CONVENTION whatever is chosen.

## 2. Late Heavy Bombardment

**Date.** Commonly quoted as ~4.1 to 3.8 Ga with a peak at ~3.9 Ga. The current best statement is looser: lunar melt rocks and meteorite shock ages "point toward a discrete episode of elevated impact flux between ~3.5 and ~4.0–4.2 Ga, and a relative quiescence between ~4.0–4.2 and ~4.4 Ga" (Bottke & Norman 2017 [abs]); the large basins Imbrium and Orientale formed 3.9 to 3.7 Gyr ago (Morbidelli et al. 2018 [abs]); a tail of large impacts on Earth lasted to ~2.5–2.0 Ga (Bottke & Norman 2017 [abs]). A sharp spike at 3.9 Ga is not established.

**Key sources**
- Tera F, Papanastassiou DA, Wasserburg GJ (1974). Isotopic evidence for a terminal lunar cataclysm. *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 22(1): 1–21. doi:10.1016/0012-821X(74)90059-4. (Founding paper.)
- Bottke WF, Norman MD (2017). The Late Heavy Bombardment. *Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences* 45(1): 619–647. doi:10.1146/annurev-earth-063016-020131. (Best review.)
- Boehnke P, Harrison TM (2016). Illusory Late Heavy Bombardments. *PNAS* 113(39): 10802–10806. doi:10.1073/pnas.1611535113. (Strongest statement of the case against a spike.)
- Morbidelli A, Nesvorný D, Laurenz V, Marchi S, Rubie DC, Elkins-Tanton L, Wieczorek M, Jacobson S (2018). The timeline of the lunar bombardment: revisited. *Icarus* 305: 262–276. doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2017.12.046.

**Other sources used**
- Cohen BA, Swindle TD, Kring DA (2000). Support for the lunar cataclysm hypothesis from lunar meteorite impact melt ages. *Science* 290(5497): 1754–1756. doi:10.1126/science.290.5497.1754.
- Gomes R, Levison HF, Tsiganis K, Morbidelli A (2005). Origin of the cataclysmic Late Heavy Bombardment period of the terrestrial planets. *Nature* 435(7041): 466–469. doi:10.1038/nature03676.
- Zellner NEB (2017). Cataclysm no more: new views on the timing and delivery of lunar impactors. *Origins of Life and Evolution of Biospheres* 47(3): 261–280. doi:10.1007/s11084-017-9536-3.
- Nesvorný D, Vokrouhlický D, Bottke WF, Levison HF (2018). Evidence for very early migration of the Solar System planets from the Patroclus–Menoetius binary Jupiter Trojan. *Nature Astronomy* 2(11): 878–882. doi:10.1038/s41550-018-0564-3.
- Hartmann WK (2019). History of the terminal cataclysm paradigm: epistemology of a planetary bombardment that never (?) happened. *Geosciences* 9(7): 285. doi:10.3390/geosciences9070285.
- Marchi S, Bottke WF, Elkins-Tanton LT, Bierhaus M, Wuennemann K, Morbidelli A, Kring DA (2014). Widespread mixing and burial of Earth's Hadean crust by asteroid impacts. *Nature* 511(7511): 578–582. doi:10.1038/nature13539.

- Hiesinger H, Head JW, Wolf U, Jaumann R, Neukum G (2011). Ages and stratigraphy of lunar mare basalts: a synthesis. In *Recent Advances and Current Research Issues in Lunar Stratigraphy*, GSA Special Paper 477. doi:10.1130/2011.2477(01). (Book chapter; Crossref gives no pages.)

**The dispute, laid out**

*For a spike (the "terminal cataclysm").*
- Apollo samples showed widespread resetting of isotopic clocks near 3.9 Ga (Tera et al. 1974, title verified; founding claim [mem]).
- Lunar meteorites, a more random sample of the Moon than Apollo, contain no impact melt older than 3.92 Ga; the authors called this "an anomalous spike" (Cohen et al. 2000 [abs]).
- A mechanism was supplied: late migration of the giant planets scattering comets and asteroids inward (the "Nice model", Gomes et al. 2005 [abs]).
- If the Moon's mantle records everything it accreted, "only the cataclysm scenario can explain the data", beginning ~3.95 Gyr ago (Morbidelli et al. 2018 [abs], first half of their argument).

*Against a spike (the "accretion tail").*
- Argon ages are easily partly reset; modelling shows that "monotonically declining impact histories yield apparent age peaks that could be misinterpreted" (Boehnke & Harrison 2016 [abs]).
- Reviewing all lines of evidence, "most evidence supports a prolonged lunar (and thus, terrestrial) bombardment from ~4.2 to 3.4 Ga and not a cataclysmic spike at ~3.9 Ga" (Zellner 2017 [abs]).
- If the Moon's mantle lost its early siderophile elements during magma-ocean crystallisation, the 3 to 4 Gyr crater record "can be explained in the accretion tail scenario" (Morbidelli et al. 2018 [abs], second half).
- The giant-planet instability now appears to have happened very early, which removes the mechanism for a late spike (Nesvorný et al. 2018, title verified; the "within ~100 Myr" figure is [mem]).
- Apollo landing sites may all be dominated by debris from one basin, Imbrium ([mem]; discussed in Bottke & Norman 2017 and Zellner 2017, not confirmed from their abstracts).

*Middle position.* "The most parsimonious solution ... is a hybrid model" with an early declining population plus a later instability-driven one (Bottke & Norman 2017 [abs]). Hartmann 2019 (title verified) is a history of how the spike idea rose and fell.

**What is known**
- Moon, Mars and Mercury were heavily cratered in their first billion years; timing and sources "are controversial" (Bottke & Norman 2017 [abs]).
- Liquid water and continental-type crust existed on Earth by ~4.4 Ga, from a 4,404 ± 8 Myr zircon (Wilde et al. 2001 [abs]).
- Models say large impacts repeatedly reworked the Hadean crust and that "existing oceans would have repeatedly boiled away into steam atmospheres as a result of large collisions as late as about 4 billion years ago" (Marchi et al. 2014 [abs]).
- Models also say there is "no plausible situation in which the habitable zone was fully sterilized" (Abramov & Mojzsis 2009 [abs]).
- No Earth rock from this time records the bombardment directly; the oldest rock components are 4.03 Ga (Wilde et al. 2001 [abs]). All evidence is from the Moon, meteorites and models.

**What a painter could show**
1. *Impacts on an Earth that already had oceans*: water by 4.4 Ga is EVIDENCE (zircon chemistry, Wilde et al. 2001). Frequency and size of impacts on Earth: INFERENCE scaled from the Moon. A sky full of simultaneous meteors is CONVENTION; even in the spike scenario, large impacts were separated by long intervals.
2. *The Moon*: larger than today. The oldest measurement is 3.22 Ga (70% of today's distance, 1.4 times today's width); at 3.9 Ga it was closer still by an amount only models give (Farhat et al. 2022). INFERENCE. Its face differed: Imbrium and Orientale were forming (3.9 to 3.7 Ga, Morbidelli et al. 2018 [abs]), and most of the dark lava plains that make today's "man in the Moon" erupted later (Hiesinger et al. 2011 [mem]). A present-day Moon face in this scene is CONVENTION.
3. *Day*: shorter than 13 hours (13 h is measured at 3.22 Ga). INFERENCE.
4. *Land and sky*: bare volcanic rock, no life visible. Extent of land above water is unknown. Sky colour unknown; a blue sky is a fair default for a clear nitrogen–CO2 atmosphere, an orange or red sky is CONVENTION.
5. *Sun*: about 75% of today's brightness. INFERENCE.

## 3. Earliest signs of life

**Date.** Widely accepted evidence begins at ~3.48 Ga (Dresser Formation, Pilbara, Western Australia) and is strongest at 3.43 Ga (Strelley Pool Formation, same region). "About 3.5 billion years ago" is defensible; anything older must be flagged as disputed.

**What is known (widely accepted)**
- *Dresser Formation, ~3.48 Ga.* It is "well known for hosting some of Earth's earliest convincing evidence of life (stromatolites, fractionated sulfur/carbon isotopes, microfossils)" in a volcanic caldera with hot-water circulation (Djokic et al. 2017 [abs]). Nutman et al. 2016 [abs], while arguing for something older, call it "the previous most convincing and generally accepted multidisciplinary evidence for oldest life".
- Drill core from below the weathered zone shows Dresser stromatolites with organic matter preserved in pyrite, including strands read as biofilm remains, with δ13C of −29.6 ± 0.3‰ (Baumgartner et al. 2019 [abs]).
- Hot-spring deposits on land (geyserite, sinter terraces) with stromatolites and bubble textures occur in the same formation (Djokic et al. 2017 [abs]).
- *Strelley Pool Formation, 3.43 Ga.* A shallow carbonate platform kilometres across with seven kinds of stromatolite; the authors "refute contemporary abiogenic hypotheses" on the grounds of diversity, complexity and setting (Allwood et al. 2006 [abs]). The same formation yielded sulphur-metabolising microfossils (Wacey et al. 2011, title verified).
- Chemical traces at ~3.5 Ga: sulphur isotopes read as microbial sulphate reduction (Shen et al. 2001, title verified) and methane in fluid inclusions read as microbial (Ueno et al. 2006, title verified).

**What is debated**
- *Even the Pilbara stromatolites were once doubted.* Lowe (1994, title verified) argued that all described stromatolites older than 3.2 Ga were non-biological. Allwood et al. 2006 and Baumgartner et al. 2019 are the main answers. Acceptance is broad, not universal.
- *Apex chert filaments, ~3.465 Ga.* Described as eleven kinds of filamentous microbe (Schopf 1993 [abs]); reinterpreted as "secondary artefacts formed from amorphous graphite" in hydrothermal veins (Brasier et al. 2002 [abs]); defended again with isotope measurements (Schopf et al. 2018, title verified). Unresolved. Do not present these as the oldest fossils without the caveat.
- *Isua, Greenland, 3.7 Ga structures.* Reported as 1 to 4 cm stromatolites (Nutman et al. 2016 [abs]); re-examined and found to be "more plausibly ... deformation structures formed ... long after burial" (Allwood et al. 2018 [abs]; also Zawaski et al. 2020, title verified).
- *Isua and Akilia carbon, 3.7 to 3.8+ Ga.* Isotopically light graphite (Rosing 1999; Mojzsis et al. 1996, titles verified). The Akilia claim was challenged on the origin of the host rock (Fedo & Whitehouse 2002; Lepland et al. 2005, titles verified). Rosing's Isua graphite is regarded as the more robust of the two [mem].
- *Labrador graphite, ≥3.95 Ga* (Tashiro et al. 2017 [abs]); age and setting challenged (Whitehouse et al. 2019, title verified).
- *Nuvvuagittuq, Quebec, tubes "at least 3,770 million and possibly 4,280 million years old"* (Dodd et al. 2017 [abs]); similar tubes can be grown without life (McMahon 2019, title verified).
- *Jack Hills zircon, 4.1 Ga.* One zircon out of more than 10,000 holds graphite with δ13C of −24 ± 5‰, "consistent with a biogenic origin" (Bell et al. 2015 [abs]); the authors' own title says "potentially".

**What a painter could show**
1. *Strelley Pool reef*: a shallow tidal shelf of pale carbonate with low cones, domes and wavy-laminated mounds (seven forms, Allwood et al. 2006 [abs]). EVIDENCE for forms and setting. Individual structures are centimetres to decimetres across [mem]; check the paper's figures for scale. The living mats' colour (green, purple, brown) is CONVENTION taken from modern mats.
2. *Dresser hot springs*: a low volcanic caldera with steaming pools, geyser mounds and sinter terraces on land beside shallow water (Djokic et al. 2017 [abs]). EVIDENCE for the features; their appearance is INFERENCE from modern hot springs.
3. *Moon and day*: the nearest measurement is 3.22 Ga: Moon at ~70% of today's distance (about 1.4 times as wide, twice the disc area), day ~13 hours (Eulenfeld & Heubeck 2023 [abs]). At 3.48 Ga slightly more extreme. EVIDENCE at 3.22 Ga, INFERENCE for 3.48 Ga. Tides were stronger and came about twice as often as today: INFERENCE.
4. *Sea and sky*: no free oxygen. Deep water was iron-rich (Poulton & Canfield 2011, cited under event 4). A greenish sea has been proposed from modelling of light in iron-bearing water (Matsuo et al. 2025 [abs], cited under event 4): one hypothesis, INFERENCE at best. The sky: a nitrogen–CO2 atmosphere scatters blue light as ours does; evidence for orange methane haze exists only for much later (2.7 to 2.5 Ga, see event 4). Blue sky = INFERENCE; orange sky at 3.5 Ga = CONVENTION.
5. *Land*: bare rock and volcanic ash, no soil, no plants. Microbial films on land are thought probable only from ~3.0 Ga (Lenton & Daines 2017 [abs], cited under event 4), apart from the Dresser hot springs. INFERENCE.

## 4. Great Oxidation Event (GOE)

**Date.** Not a single moment. Current reading:
- First loss of the sulphur-isotope signal of an oxygen-free atmosphere: between 2,501.5 ± 1.7 Ma and 2,434 ± 6.6 Ma in Fennoscandia (Warke et al. 2020 [abs]); onset "between ca. 2,460 and 2,426 Ma" in South Africa (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]). Detrital pyrite and uraninite still present at 2.415 Ga in one South African unit (Johnson et al. 2014 [abs]) shows the fit is not perfect.
- Oxygen then "fluctuated across the threshold of 10⁻⁵ of the present atmospheric level for about 200 million years, with permanent atmospheric oxygenation finally arriving ... at about 2.22 billion years ago" (Poulton et al. 2021 [abs]).
- Older single-step datings: by 2.32 Ga (Bekker et al. 2004 [abs]); 2.33 Ga, completed within 1 to 10 Myr (Luo et al. 2016 [abs]). These are now read as one of several crossings.
- Some authors use "GOE" for the whole interval to the end of the Lomagundi excursion at ~2.06 Ga (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]; "ca. 2,400 to 2,050 Ma" in Hodgskiss et al. 2019 [abs]).

Recommended: "began about 2.4 billion years ago and took some 200 million years to become permanent".

**What is known**
- Before the event, atmospheric oxygen was below about 10⁻⁵ of today's level; the marker is the disappearance of mass-independent sulphur isotope fractionation (Bekker et al. 2004 [abs]; Poulton et al. 2021 [abs]; Pavlov & Kasting 2002, title verified).
- Before the event, river sands carried grains of pyrite and uraninite that oxygen destroys; these imply less than 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ atm of O2 and "effectively vanish from the sedimentary record" afterwards (Johnson et al. 2014 [abs]).
- After it: red beds, oxidised ancient soils and the return of sulphate deposits (Kopp et al. 2005 [abs], cited under event 5).
- The transition is tangled with the Paleoproterozoic glaciations (event 5) and was oscillatory (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]; Poulton et al. 2021 [abs]).
- Cyanobacteria produced the oxygen (Matsuo et al. 2025 [abs] states this as the standard view).

**What is debated**
- *When oxygen-producing photosynthesis evolved*: by ~3.0 Ga, half a billion years early (Planavsky et al. 2014, title verified), or only shortly before the event (Kopp et al. 2005 [abs]; Fischer et al. 2016, title verified).
- *"Whiffs" of oxygen at 2.5 Ga* (Anbar et al. 2007) versus a re-examination finding an anoxic ocean (Slotznick et al. 2022) (titles verified).
- *Cause of the delay and the rise*: change in volcanic gases (Holland 2002, title verified); loss of hydrogen to space, which "oxidized the Earth" (Catling & Zahnle 2020 [abs]); lengthening days boosting oxygen export from microbial mats (Klatt et al. 2021 [abs], offered as a "possible contribution").
- *Tempo*: one rapid step (Luo et al. 2016) versus 200 Myr of oscillation (Poulton et al. 2021).
- *A caution*: the popular "oxygen catastrophe" that poisoned most life has no fossil evidence behind it that I could find; I found no primary source documenting an extinction. Treat it as CONVENTION if it appears in any draft.

**What a painter could show**
1. *The land changed colour.* Before: greys, blacks and browns of unoxidised rock, with rivers carrying glittering pyrite (EVIDENCE: detrital pyrite, Johnson et al. 2014). After: rust-red sediments (EVIDENCE: red beds, Kopp et al. 2005 [abs]). This is the one visible signature of the event. Rivers were wide, shallow and braided with no vegetation to hold banks (Gibling & Davies 2012, title verified; the sheet-braided point is [mem]).
2. *Sky.* Oxygen does not make the sky blue; nitrogen does. So the event itself did not change sky colour: INFERENCE from physics. Before the event there is geochemical evidence for episodes of hydrocarbon haze, each lasting about a million years, at ~2.7 to 2.5 Ga (Zerkle et al. 2012, title verified; Izon et al. 2017 [abs]); modelling of such haze gives a "pale orange dot" (Arney et al. 2016 [abs]). An orange sky *before* and a blue sky *after* is INFERENCE, hedged; a permanently orange Archean sky is CONVENTION.
3. *Sea.* Shallow water gained oxygen; deep water stayed iron-rich for much of Earth history (Poulton & Canfield 2011 [abs]). Sea colour: green tint proposed from modelling (Matsuo et al. 2025 [abs]); otherwise unknown. INFERENCE at best; red or "rusting" seas are CONVENTION.
4. *Life visible*: stromatolites and microbial mats in shallows; mats on wet land probable ("from c. 3.0 billion yr ago onwards", Lenton & Daines 2017 [abs]). INFERENCE for land mats.
5. *Moon and day*: at 2.46 Ga, day 16.9 ± 0.2 h and Moon at 321,800 ± 6,500 km (Lantink et al. 2022 [abs]): about 1.19 times today's width (my arithmetic). EVIDENCE. Sun ~83% of today.

## 5. Huronian glaciations

**Date.** ~2.45 to 2.22 Ga for the whole episode. The name comes from the Huronian Supergroup of Ontario, which holds three glacial units (Kopp et al. 2005 [abs] for "three"; the names Ramsay Lake, Bruce and Gowganda are [mem]). The Canadian rocks are bracketed between volcanic rocks of ~2.45 Ga at the base and 2.22 Ga intrusions that cut them (Corfu & Andrews 1986 for the 2.22 Ga Nipissing intrusions [mem]; the two bracket ages match Bekker et al. 2004 [abs], "before 2.45 ... by 2.22 Gyr ago"). Tuff beds dated in both Canada and South Africa "establish the existence of four glaciations between 2.45 and 2.22 Ga", with the sulphur-isotope rise in oxygen falling "between the second and third glaciations" (Rasmussen et al. 2013; wording as given in index listings of the abstract found by web search, not read on the publisher's page). The individual tuff ages (~2.31 Ga) are [mem]. In South Africa the low-latitude Makganyene glaciation fell "between ca. 2,460 and 2,426 Ma", fixed by a 2,426 ± 3 Ma age on the Ongeluk lavas, and climatic instability continued "until ≤2,250–2,240 Ma" (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]).

**What is known**
- There were at least three separate glaciations in this interval (Bekker et al. 2004 [abs]; Kopp et al. 2005 [abs]).
- At least one reached sea level near the equator. The Ongeluk lavas, which lie on and interfinger with the Makganyene glacial deposit ([mem] for the field relation), give a palaeolatitude of 11° ± 6° (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]; first shown by Evans et al. 1997).
- *Relation to the GOE.* The sulphur-isotope signal of rising oxygen appears below the glacial beds in Fennoscandia, 60 m beneath them: "the S-MIF/S-MDF transition preceded the Paleoproterozoic snowball Earth" (Warke et al. 2020 [abs]). In South Africa the 2.32 Ga oxygenated shales lie "between what are probably the second and third of the three Palaeoproterozoic glacial events" (Bekker et al. 2004 [abs]). Oxygen kept oscillating across the last two glaciations (Poulton et al. 2021 [abs]). So oxygen rise and glaciation are interleaved, with first oxygen coming before the low-latitude glaciation.
- The glaciation was followed in South Africa by the huge Kalahari manganese deposit, which needs free oxygen to form (Kirschvink et al. 2000 [abs]).
- The Ongeluk eruptions were part of a large igneous province (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]).

**What is debated**
- *Cause.* The leading idea is that new oxygen destroyed a methane greenhouse; a model shows this "could have ... triggered a snowball event on time-scales as short as 1 million years" (Kopp et al. 2005 [abs]). It is a hypothesis. Others stress large volcanic provinces (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]) or rift-related uplift (Young 2019, title verified).
- *Correlation and order.* Kopp et al. 2005 placed the Makganyene snowball at ~2.3–2.2 Ga, *after* all three Canadian glaciations; Gumsley et al. 2017 moved it ~100 Myr earlier, making it one of the first. Hoffman 2013 and Rasmussen et al. 2013 give other correlations. How the Canadian and South African glaciations match one-to-one is unsettled, as is the total number: three (Bekker et al. 2004 [abs]) or four (Rasmussen et al. 2013, as above).
- *How global.* Low-latitude evidence exists only for the Makganyene. Calling all the Huronian glaciations "snowball Earth" goes beyond the evidence.

**What a painter could show**
1. *Ice at tropical sea level*: EVIDENCE for one glaciation (palaeomagnetism, 11° ± 6°). A wholly white planet: INFERENCE for the Makganyene, CONVENTION for the others.
2. *What the rocks record*: ice-rafted stones dropped into finely layered sea-floor mud, and boulder-clay (diamictite) ([mem], Gowganda Formation; Young et al. 2001, title verified). EVIDENCE of floating ice and open or seasonally open water at those places.
3. *Lava and ice together*: flood-basalt eruptions (Ongeluk) at about the time of the low-latitude ice (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]); the lavas erupted under water ([mem]). Fire-and-ice scenery is INFERENCE.
4. *Land and life*: bare rock and ice; no visible life on land. Land surface reddening had begun or was beginning (see event 4).
5. *Moon and day*: as event 4 (day ~17 h, Moon ~1.19 times today's width): EVIDENCE at 2.46 Ga, which is almost exactly this time.

## 6. Lomagundi–Jatuli carbon isotope excursion (LJE)

**Date.** Onset between ~2.31 and ~2.22 Ga; end between ~2.11 and ~2.06 Ga; duration therefore somewhere between about 130 and 250 Myr (Martin et al. 2013 [mem] for the bracketing ages; the duration range is confirmed by Hodgskiss et al. 2023 [abs], "ca. 130–250 million years"). Other statements: "between 2.3–2.1 Ga" (Prave et al. 2022 [abs]); onset "at about 2.22 billion years ago" (Poulton et al. 2021 [abs]); end "ca. 2,060 Ma" (Gumsley et al. 2017 [abs]). Size: carbonate δ13C of about +5 to +30‰, against a normal value near 0 (Hodgskiss et al. 2023 [abs]).

**What is known**
- Carbonate rocks of this age on several continents are strongly enriched in carbon-13; it is "ostensibly the largest magnitude ... longest duration ... positive carbon isotope excursion" in Earth history (Hodgskiss et al. 2023 [abs]).
- *Classical meaning for life and oxygen.* Heavy carbonate carbon implies unusually high burial of organic matter, which leaves oxygen behind; the LJE was "the longest-lived positive δ13C excursion in Earth history, generating a huge oxygen source to the atmosphere" (Canfield et al. 2013 [abs]). The "oxygen overshoot" idea is from Bekker & Holland 2012 (title verified). Karhu & Holland's estimate that the oxygen released was many times today's atmospheric stock is [mem].
- *Evaporites and sulphate.* A ~2.0 Ga evaporite succession ~800 m thick in the Onega Basin, Karelia, has ~100 m of halite, ~500 m of anhydrite–magnesite and ~200 m of dolomite–magnesite; it requires seawater sulphate of at least 10 mmol/kg, an oxidant reservoir "equivalent to more than 20% of the modern ocean-atmosphere oxidizing capacity" (Blättler et al. 2018 [abs]). Sulphate evaporites also occur in the ~2.2–2.1 Ga Lucknow Formation, South Africa (Schröder et al. 2008, title verified). Sulphate needs oxygen, so these are independent evidence of an oxidised surface.
- *Phosphorites.* "The oldest known phosphogenic event occurred around 2.0 Ga" (Papineau 2010 [abs]); phosphate-rich beds in Karelia have been linked to sulphur bacteria (Lepland et al. 2014, title verified).
- *Aftermath.* In Gabon, sediments from ~2,150 to 2,080 Ma go from well-oxygenated deep water to sulphidic water; as the excursion waned, "the oxygen source became a net oxygen sink" and low oxygen "may have persisted for 200 million years" (Canfield et al. 2013 [abs]; also Ossa Ossa et al. 2018, title verified). Sulphate minerals of ~2,018 Ma record "a rapid reduction in primary productivity of >80%" (Hodgskiss et al. 2019 [abs]). Against a simple crash, Mänd et al. 2020 (title verified) argue for oxygenated oceans after the LJE.
- *Shunga.* After the positive excursion comes a negative one, the "Shunga-Francevillian anomaly", read as "intense oxidative weathering of rocks" (Kump et al. 2011 [abs]). The ~2.0 Ga Karelian shungite rocks are metamorphosed oil shale and record petroleum generation (Melezhik et al. 1999, title verified); they are extremely carbon-rich black rocks [mem].
- *Oklo.* The natural nuclear reactors of Gabon lie in the same Francevillian basin and are tied by their discoverers to atmospheric evolution, since uranium only becomes mobile in oxygenated water (Gauthier-Lafaye & Weber 2003, title verified; mechanism [mem]).

**What is debated**
- *Global or local?* A compilation of 14,943 measurements finds the heavy values "limited largely to nearshore-marine and coastal-evaporitic settings" (means +6.2 and +8.1‰) while open-marine settings stayed at +1.5 ± 2.4‰; the LJE "cannot be construed a priori as representative of the global carbon cycle ... nor as direct evidence for oxygenation" (Prave et al. 2022 [abs]). The Gabon section shows the same dependence on water depth (Bakakas Mayika et al. 2020 [abs]). Hodgskiss et al. 2023 [abs] agree the data "call into question" a globally synchronous excursion. This is a live reversal of the textbook account; write with care.
- *Francevillian "fossils".* Pyritised structures up to 12 cm across in 2.1 Ga black shale of Gabon were interpreted as "colonial organisms" with coordinated growth (El Albani et al. 2010 [abs]); later, string-shaped structures up to 6 mm wide and 170 mm long were read as traces of a moving organism, compared with slime-mould slugs (El Albani et al. 2019 [abs]). The discoverers call evidence for early macroscopic life "controversial" in their own abstract. The accompanying *Nature* commentary (Donoghue & Antcliffe 2010, title verified) was cautious about what kind of organism, if any, is represented [mem]. Informal scepticism holds that they may be mineral growths or microbial-mat structures; **I could not find and verify a peer-reviewed paper making that case**, so the critique is unsourced here. No claim of "first animals" or "first multicellular life" is safe. They are, at most, large structures of probable biological origin whose makers are unknown. A 2024 paper from the same group ties them to local nutrient enrichment (Chi Fru et al. 2024, title verified).
- *What ended it* and whether oxygen fell afterwards (Canfield 2013; Hodgskiss 2019) or stayed high (Mänd 2020).

**What a painter could show**
1. *A salt coast*: tidal flats and shallow lagoons crusted with salt and gypsum, with magnesite and dolomite muds, in Karelia ~2.0 Ga. EVIDENCE (Blättler et al. 2018). Red sediment nearby: EVIDENCE of red beds generally after the GOE (event 4).
2. *Stromatolite shallows*: the heavy-carbon rocks are shallow-water carbonates, many built by microbial mats ([mem]). EVIDENCE for stromatolites; mat colours CONVENTION.
3. *The Gabon structures*: lobed, wrinkled discs up to 12 cm with radial fabric on a muddy sea floor (El Albani et al. 2010 [abs]). The shapes are EVIDENCE; showing them as living organisms is INFERENCE that many specialists do not accept; showing them moving is INFERENCE on INFERENCE. Label accordingly.
4. *The Shunga aftermath*: black, organic-rich muds and oil seeping at the sea floor (Melezhik et al. 1999). INFERENCE from the rocks.
5. *Day, Moon, Sun*: between the 16.9 h measured at 2.46 Ga and the ~19 h mid-Proterozoic plateau; Moon a little under 1.19 times today's width; Sun ~85%. INFERENCE (interpolation). Land bare; sky blue by default (INFERENCE).

## 7. Snowball Earth: Sturtian and Marinoan glaciations

**Date.**
- *Sturtian*: began ~717 Ma, ended ~660 Ma, about 57 Myr. Onset: a 716.5 Ma tuff within glacial deposits in Yukon (Macdonald et al. 2010 [abs]); pre-glacial tuffs of 719.68 ± 0.46 Ma in Ethiopia, "consistent with synchronous global onset ... at ca. 717 Ma" (MacLennan et al. 2018 [abs]). End: 659.0 ± 4.5 Ma (Re-Os) directly above the diamictite in Mongolia (Rooney et al. 2015 [abs]); 658.80 ± 0.50 Ma within the cap carbonate in South China, a minimum age for the end (Zhou et al. 2019 [abs]).
- *Marinoan*: began after 651.69 ± 0.64 Ma (Nelson et al. 2020 [abs]) and before 639.29 ± 0.26 Ma (Prave et al. 2016 [abs]); ended at ~635 Ma: 635.21 ± 0.59 Ma (Prave et al. 2016 [abs]), 634.57 ± 0.88 and 635.23 ± 0.57 Ma (Zhou et al. 2019 [abs]; Condon et al. 2005 [abs]). Duration at least 4 Myr and less than 17 Myr (Prave 2016; Nelson 2020 [abs]). Some papers write "654–635 Ma" (Ye et al. 2015; Song et al. 2023 [abs]); the 654 Ma figure is an older, looser constraint and is superseded by Nelson et al. 2020.
- The ice-free gap between them lasted at least 9 and at most ~20 Myr (Nelson 2020; Prave 2016 [abs]).
- The Cryogenian Period is defined as ~720 to 635 Ma (Shields-Zhou et al. 2016, title).

**What is known**
- Ice was "grounded below sea level at very low paleolatitudes": Sturtian glacial deposits in Yukon are dated at 716.5 Ma, when Laurentia sat on the equator (Macdonald et al. 2010 [abs]). Low-latitude glacial deposits in South Australia had been shown earlier (Embleton & Williams 1986, title verified).
- Both glaciations were long and each began and ended at the same time on several continents (Rooney et al. 2015 [abs]; Zhou et al. 2019 [abs]). An earlier supposed "Kaigas" glaciation at ~740 Ma is refuted (Rooney et al. 2015 [abs]).
- Both end abruptly beneath a "cap carbonate"; the Marinoan cap dolostone formed in under a million years (Zhou et al. 2019 [abs]). The proposed mechanism is escape by volcanic CO2 building up to "about 350 times the modern level" (Hoffman et al. 1998 [abs]).
- Life got through. Seaweed-like fossils occur in black shales between Marinoan glacial beds in South China, so there were "episodes of open waters" in the sunlit zone at mid latitudes (Ye et al. 2015 [abs]; Song et al. 2023 [abs]).
- A third, later glaciation, the Gaskiers at ~580 Ma, lasted at most ~340,000 years and was not a snowball (Pu et al. 2016 [abs]).

**What is debated**
- *Hard snowball or open water?* A fully ice-covered ocean (Hoffman et al. 1998; 2017) versus solutions with open tropical water (Hyde et al. 2000; the "Jormungand" state of Abbot et al. 2011; titles verified). The Marinoan fossils and "at least intermittent open-water conditions" 4 Myr before its end (Prave et al. 2016 [abs]) are used by both sides.
- *Trigger.* Weathering of fresh flood basalts at low latitude (Cox et al. 2016, title verified); the onset coincides with the Franklin volcanic province (Macdonald et al. 2010 [abs]).
- *Where life survived.* Dusty meltwater ponds on the ice ("cryoconite pans", Hoffman 2016 [abs]); open coastal water (Song et al. 2023 [abs]).
- *Effect on evolution.* A shift to algae-dominated oceans between the two glaciations is argued from molecular fossils (Brocks et al. 2017, title verified); its link to animals is contested [mem].

**What a painter could show**
1. *Sea ice at the equator*: EVIDENCE for ice reaching tropical sea level. A fully frozen ocean: INFERENCE. Models give floating ice hundreds of metres thick that flowed slowly toward the equator as a "sea glacier" (Hoffman 2016 [abs]); tropical ice more than 100 m thick unless the surface was warmer than about −12 °C (Warren et al. 2002 [abs]).
2. *Not all white.* Models imply that "Snowball continents never lacked ice-free areas"; wind-blown dust and volcanic ash collected on the ice, darkening the equatorial zone and forming ponds metres to tens of metres across, an "ablation surface 60 million km² in area" (Hoffman 2016 [abs]). Dirty, pond-pocked ice and bare brown mountains are INFERENCE (models plus modern glacier analogues). The spotless white ball seen from space is CONVENTION.
3. *Life*: green or dark microbial scum in meltwater ponds (INFERENCE, Hoffman 2016); seaweeds on the sea bed where water was open (EVIDENCE of the seaweeds, Ye et al. 2015; their setting is INFERENCE).
4. *Volcanoes* venting through or beside ice sheets: required by the CO2 escape mechanism (Hoffman et al. 1998 [abs]). INFERENCE.
5. *Day and Moon*: 21.9 ± 0.4 hours; about 400 days in a year; Moon at 96.5% of today's distance, so to the eye the same size as now. EVIDENCE, and it comes from tidal layers in the Elatina Formation, part of the Marinoan glacial succession itself (Williams 2000 [abs]; Williams dated it ~620 Ma, the Marinoan is now known to end at 635 Ma). Sun ~94%.
6. *Afterwards*: a very hot, CO2-rich greenhouse and rapidly deposited pale carbonate on the sea floor (Hoffman et al. 1998 [abs]). INFERENCE for the climate, EVIDENCE for the carbonate.

## 8. Shuram carbon isotope excursion

**Date.** Two current estimates:
- 574.0 ± 4.7 Ma to 567.3 ± 3.0 Ma, from Re-Os dates bracketing the excursion on two palaeocontinents (Oman and north-west Canada) (Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]).
- 570.2 ± 1.1 Ma to 562.5 ± 1.1 Ma, duration 7.7 ± 0.2 Myr, from counting orbital cycles in an Oman well log; a fall to the lowest values in ~1 Myr, then 6 to 7 Myr of recovery (Gong & Li 2020 [abs]).

They agree on a duration of roughly 7 Myr and overlap within error at the start. Older literature placed it at ~560–551 Ma or stretched it over tens of millions of years (Condon et al. 2005 [abs] for a ~551 Ma excursion; Le Guerroué et al. 2006, title: "50 Myr recovery"); the ~551 Ma event is now treated as a separate, younger excursion that "ended ca. 550 Ma" (Yang et al. 2021 [abs]). Size: carbonate δ13C falls to about −12‰, the lowest known (Gong & Li 2020 [abs]; Husson et al. 2020 [abs]).

**What is known**
- The excursion is found in Oman, South Australia (Wonoka Formation), South China, north-west Canada and the western United States (Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]; Gong & Li 2020 [abs]; Husson et al. 2015, title).
- It has the same duration wherever measured and the same age within error on two continents (Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]; Gong & Li 2020 [abs]; Minguez & Kodama 2017, title verified).
- It follows the Gaskiers glaciation (580.90 to 579.24 Ma, Pu et al. 2016 [abs]) (Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]).

**The dispute, laid out**

*It is a primary signal of the ocean.*
- Proposed cause: oxidation of a very large store of dissolved organic carbon in the deep ocean (Rothman et al. 2003, title verified; Fike et al. 2006 [abs], who tie it to a stage of ocean oxygenation).
- Synchrony and equal duration worldwide "support the interpretation that the Shuram is a primary and synchronous event" (Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]; Gong & Li 2020 [abs]).

*It was produced or distorted in the rock afterwards.*
- There was not enough oxidant in the Ediacaran world to burn that much carbon (Bristow & Kennedy 2008, title verified; argument [mem]).
- Burial alteration by fluids (Derry 2010, title verified); alteration by rain-fed groundwater carrying land-derived carbon (Knauth & Kennedy 2009, title verified; argument [mem]); carbonate that grew within the sediment (Schrag et al. 2013, title verified).
- Matching carbon-isotope curves in different basins do not prove an ocean origin; young rocks altered by fresh water during sea-level falls show the same matching (Swart & Kennedy 2012, title verified; argument [mem]).

*Where it stands now: real and early, but not a simple whole-ocean signal.*
- Grain-by-grain measurements in South Australia find "little evidence for the hypothesis that a late diagenetic overprint has generated" the excursion, but do find a steep gradient: shallow-water carbonate at −12‰ and deeper or pore-water carbonate up to +5‰. The authors conclude "a still-unknown global process" acted on shallow water (Husson et al. 2020 [abs]).
- In Oman, four kinds of alteration are identified, yet "the degree of alteration is insufficient to account for" the trends (Bergmann et al. 2025 [abs]).
- Busch et al. 2022 (title: "Global and local drivers") and Wostbrock & Planavsky 2026 (title: "does not record open ocean conditions") point the same way: a synchronous event expressed in shallow shelf water, not a measure of the whole ocean's carbon. Details of those two papers are not confirmed from abstracts.
- Consequence for wording: avoid "the ocean's carbon changed" and avoid "an artefact". "The largest carbon-isotope anomaly known, global and simultaneous in shallow seas, cause unknown" is supportable.

**Timing relative to Ediacaran organisms**
- Start of the excursion (574.0 ± 4.7 Ma, Rooney et al. 2020) is indistinguishable from the oldest architecturally complex Ediacaran fossils in Newfoundland (574.17 ± 0.66 Ma, Matthews et al. 2021 [abs]); macrofossils in north-west Canada appear at the same time within error (Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]).
- It ended (567.3 ± 3.0 or 562.5 ± 1.1 Ma) before the White Sea assemblage (~560–550 Ma; *Kimberella* older than 555.3 ± 0.3 Ma, Martin et al. 2000 [abs]). It "precedes a marked increase in the diversity and complexity" of macroscopic organisms (Bergmann et al. 2025 [abs]).
- So the Avalon communities lived through it; the White Sea and Nama communities came after. On Gong & Li's dates the famous Mistaken Point "E" surface (565.00 ± 0.64 Ma, Matthews et al. 2021 [abs]) falls inside the excursion; on Rooney et al.'s dates it falls just after. This cannot yet be resolved.
- A second, younger negative excursion ending ~550 Ma coincides with the White Sea to Nama turnover (Yang et al. 2021 [abs]).
- Whether the excursion *caused* anything biological is open ("invoked as a driving mechanism ... possibly linked with evolutionary innovation or extinction", Rooney et al. 2020 [abs]).

**What a painter could show**
1. *Nothing visible marks the event itself.* An isotope shift has no appearance. Any colour change of sea or sky is CONVENTION.
2. *The setting of the evidence*: shallow shelf seas depositing carbonate and silt (EVIDENCE: Oman, South Australia). Sea-floor life in such shallows at this date: microbial mats; no large organisms are known from shallow water this early (INFERENCE from the fossil record, see event 9).
3. *What was alive elsewhere*: deep-water frond communities of the Avalon assemblage were appearing at this time (EVIDENCE, Matthews et al. 2021). A diptych of shallow lifeless-looking shelf and deep-water fronds would be accurate; putting them in one view is CONVENTION.
4. *Land*: bare. A proposed late Precambrian "greening" by microbes or fungi (Knauth & Kennedy 2009) is "currently equivocal" (Lenton & Daines 2017 [abs]). Green land is CONVENTION.
5. *Day and Moon*: ~22 hours; Moon visually as now (nearest EVIDENCE is ~635 Ma, Williams 2000). Sun ~95%.

## 9. Ediacaran life: Avalon, White Sea and Nama assemblages

**Date.**
- *Avalon*: ~575 to 560 Ma (Evans et al. 2022 [abs]). Oldest architecturally complex macrofossils 574.17 ± 0.66 Ma (upper Drook Formation, Newfoundland); Mistaken Point "E" surface 565.00 ± 0.64 Ma; youngest rangeomorphs there no older than 564.13 ± 0.65 Ma (Matthews et al. 2021 [abs]). First fossils appear less than 9.5 Myr after the Gaskiers glaciation (Pu et al. 2016 [abs]). Charnwood Forest, England (*Charnia* type locality): main fossil beds 561.9 ± 0.9 Ma and younger, top of the sequence ~557 Ma (Noble et al. 2015 [abs]). Wood et al. 2019 [abs] quote ~571 Ma for the oldest macrofossils, an older figure superseded by Matthews et al.
- *White Sea*: ~560 to 550 Ma (Evans et al. 2022 [abs]). Anchor: volcanic ash at Zimnie Gory, White Sea coast, 555.3 ± 0.3 Ma, with a diverse assemblage including *Kimberella* below it (Martin et al. 2000 [abs]).
- *Nama*: ~550 to 539 Ma (Evans et al. 2022 [abs]).
- *End*: Ediacaran–Cambrian boundary at 538.6–538.8 Ma in Namibia; last Ediacaran-type fossils to first complex burrows within 410,000 years (538.99 ± 0.21 to 538.58 ± 0.19 Ma) (Linnemann et al. 2019 [abs]). Some global correlations would move the boundary up to 3 Myr younger (Bowyer et al. 2022 [abs]). The ICS chart figure of 538.8 Ma is [mem]; the chart is cited as Cohen et al. 2013 (updated) and was not re-read.
- The Ediacaran Period itself is 635 to ~539 Ma (Cantine et al. 2024 [abs]).

**What is known**
- Three assemblages, first separated by Waggoner (2003 [abs]), "remain distinct taxonomic groupings" on re-analysis (Boag et al. 2016 [abs]).
- *Avalon*: deep-water slope and basin floor, below the reach of sunlight; "soft-bodied, non-phototrophic" organisms preserved under volcanic ash on more than a hundred surfaces (Wood et al. 2003 [abs]). Dominated by rangeomorphs, fronds built of centimetre-scale units with three orders of self-similar branching (Narbonne 2004 [abs]).
- *White Sea*: shallow sandy sea floors bound by microbial mats; with no effective grazers or burrowers, mats covered the sediment, and fossils formed as "death masks" when iron minerals set the sand above a buried mat (Gehling 1999 [abs]). This assemblage has the highest diversity and the first mobile, mat-feeding animals (Evans et al. 2022 [abs]; Droser et al. 2017 [abs]).
- *Nama*: lower diversity; about 80% of White Sea genera are absent, a loss "comparable to ... Phanerozoic mass extinctions" (Evans et al. 2022 [abs]). First animals with mineral skeletons (*Cloudina*) (Penny et al. 2014 [abs]).
- At least some were animals: *Dickinsonia* fossils retain cholesterol-type molecules "found only in animals" (Bobrovskiy et al. 2018 [abs, editor's summary]); *Charnia*'s growth pattern places it as a stem-group eumetazoan (Dunn et al. 2021 [abs]); *Kimberella* is a bilaterian (see below); a cnidarian is reported from Charnwood (Dunn et al. 2022, title verified).

**What is debated**
- *Time slices or habitats?* Avalon and White Sea assemblages overlap in time at ~560–557 Ma and each has a narrow depth range, so they are partly "paleoenvironmental–ecological biotopes" (Boag et al. 2016 [abs]; Grazhdankin 2004, title; Noble et al. 2015 [abs]). The Nama is the one most clearly a distinct time stage (Boag et al. 2016 [abs]).
- *Not strictly successive.* In South China, the Shibantan limestone (~551–543 Ma) holds *Dickinsonia* (Wang et al. 2021 [abs]) and *Charnia* (Wu et al. 2024 [abs]), i.e. White Sea-type organisms at Nama-assemblage dates.
- *Why diversity fell before the Cambrian*: falling ocean oxygen (Evans et al. 2022 [abs]) versus displacement by new animals (Darroch et al. 2018, title verified; argument [mem]).
- *What they were.* Affinities of most forms remain unsettled (Xiao & Laflamme 2009, title verified; Waggoner 2003 [abs]: "no basis for classifying them all as having a single body plan").
- *Role of oxygen in their appearance*: deep-ocean oxygenation after the Gaskiers glaciation is proposed as the trigger (Canfield et al. 2007 [abs]); see also Cantine et al. 2024 [abs] on the need to reassess oxygen proxies.

**What a painter could show**
1. *Avalon scene*: a muddy, gently sloping deep-sea floor, in darkness, with fronds bent over in a current and ash settling. Setting is EVIDENCE (Wood et al. 2003). **Any light in the scene is CONVENTION**, since the sea floor lay below the sunlit zone. Label it.
2. *White Sea scene*: shallow, sunlit, rippled sand entirely covered by a leathery, wrinkled microbial mat; organisms resting on or grazing the mat; fan-shaped scratch marks. Mat and scratches are EVIDENCE (Gehling 1999; Gehling et al. 2014, see *Kimberella*). No burrow holes, no shells, no fish, no seaweed forest: EVIDENCE of absence (Gehling 1999 [abs] on absence of burrowing).
3. *Nama scene*: a shallow carbonate platform with microbial mounds and clusters of small tubes (*Cloudina*), ~548 Ma (Penny et al. 2014 [abs]). Whether the tubes formed standing reefs or were swept together after death is disputed (Mehra & Maloof 2018 [abs], cited below): upright "reef" depictions are INFERENCE.
4. *Colour*: unknown for every Ediacaran organism. All colour is CONVENTION. Height and fullness of the bodies are reconstructed from flattened impressions: INFERENCE.
5. *World*: land bare (see event 8, item 4). Day ~22 hours, Moon visually as today, Sun ~95%.

## Ediacaran organisms to draw

The site has *Charnia* and *Dickinsonia*. Recommended four: ***Kimberella***, ***Tribrachidium***, ***Fractofusus***, ***Cloudina***. Together with the existing two they give two organisms for each of the three assemblages, cover six different body plans (frond, flat quilted oval, slug-like bilaterian, three-fold disc, flat spindle, mineral tube), and each has a solid recent literature.

**Assemblage discipline: who lived with whom**

| Organism | Assemblage | With *Dickinsonia*? | With *Charnia*? |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Kimberella* | White Sea | Yes: same localities in Russia and South Australia (Gehling et al. 2014 [abs]; Evans et al. 2022, full text) | Plausible: *Charnia* occurs in White Sea-assemblage rocks ([mem], Grazhdankin 2004) |
| *Tribrachidium* | White Sea | Yes: Ediacara Member, South Australia, and White Sea ([mem] for same beds; localities confirmed by Rahman et al. 2015 and Ivantsov & Zakrevskaya 2021) | As above |
| *Fractofusus* | Avalon | **No.** Confined to Newfoundland, deep water, ~575–560 Ma (Gehling & Narbonne 2007 [abs]); *Dickinsonia* is known only from shallow-water rocks of Australia, Baltica, possibly India, and South China (Wang et al. 2021 [abs]) | Yes: both are Avalon-assemblage rangeomorphs; same surfaces at Mistaken Point ([mem]) |
| *Cloudina* | Nama | **No** known co-occurrence in any bed | **No** known co-occurrence in any bed |

**Answer to the question asked.** The two that never lived alongside *Dickinsonia* are *Fractofusus* (older, deep-water, Avalon) and *Cloudina* (younger, Nama). Of these, *Fractofusus* did live alongside *Charnia*; *Cloudina* is not known alongside either. One caveat on *Cloudina*: in South China its beds (Baimatuo Member, Liang et al. 2020 [abs]) lie directly above the Shibantan Member that holds late *Dickinsonia* and *Charnia* at ~551–543 Ma, and *Cloudina* elsewhere is as old as ~550 Ma, so their time ranges probably touch; they are still not found together. Safe groupings: **Avalon** = *Charnia* + *Fractofusus*; **White Sea** = *Dickinsonia* + *Kimberella* + *Tribrachidium*; **Nama** = *Cloudina* alone of these six.

### 1. *Kimberella quadrata*

- **Age and assemblage**: White Sea assemblage, ~560–550 Ma; Russian specimens are older than 555.3 ± 0.3 Ma (Martin et al. 2000 [abs]).
- **Localities**: White Sea coast, Russia (over 800 specimens; Fedonkin et al. 2007 [abs]); Ediacara Member, Rawnsley Quartzite, Flinders Ranges, South Australia (Gehling et al. 2014 [abs]).
- **Size**: the dorsal "shell" up to 15 cm long, 5–7 cm wide, 3–4 cm high (Fedonkin et al. 2007 [abs]). Most specimens are much smaller ([mem]).
- **Shape for an illustrator**: bilaterally symmetrical; outline an elongated oval or teardrop, like a limpet stretched lengthwise. On top, a single shield-like covering that was "stiff but flexible", not mineralised. Around the shield, a broad soft frilled or scalloped margin. Underneath, a wide flat creeping foot. From the front end, a long extensible proboscis ending in hook-like teeth, used to rake the microbial mat; it left fan-shaped sets of paired scratches in front of the body, and the animal "moved backwards" as it fed (Fedonkin et al. 2007 [abs]; Gehling et al. 2014 [abs]). It sat on the mat surface and crawled over it.
- **What it is thought to be**: a bilaterian animal; this is as secure as any Ediacaran identification ("the oldest well-documented triploblastic bilaterian", Martin et al. 2000 [abs]). Closeness to molluscs ("mollusc-like", Fedonkin & Waggoner 1997) is likely but not settled. Reconstructions differ: Ivantsov 2009 [abs] proposes a covering of hard sclerites and teeth in the mouth instead of a single shield. Draw the 2007 version and note the alternative.
- **Sources**: Fedonkin MA, Waggoner BM (1997). The Late Precambrian fossil *Kimberella* is a mollusc-like bilaterian organism. *Nature* 388(6645): 868–871. doi:10.1038/42242. — Fedonkin MA, Simonetta A, Ivantsov AY (2007). New data on *Kimberella*, the Vendian mollusc-like organism (White Sea region, Russia): palaeoecological and evolutionary implications. *Geological Society, London, Special Publications* 286(1): 157–179. doi:10.1144/SP286.12. — Gehling JG, Runnegar BN, Droser ML (2014). Scratch traces of large Ediacara bilaterian animals. *Journal of Paleontology* 88(2): 284–298. doi:10.1666/13-054. — Ivantsov AY (2009). New reconstruction of *Kimberella*, problematic Vendian metazoan. *Paleontological Journal* 43(6): 601–611. doi:10.1134/S003103010906001X.

### 2. *Tribrachidium heraldicum*

- **Age and assemblage**: White Sea assemblage; quoted as 555–550 Ma (Rahman et al. 2015 [abs]).
- **Localities**: Ediacara Member, Flinders Ranges, South Australia (Hall et al. 2015, title; Rahman et al. 2015, full text); south-eastern White Sea region, Russia (Ivantsov & Zakrevskaya 2021 [abs]). Also reported from Ukraine ([mem], unverified).
- **Size**: typically 2 to 4 cm across; fossils stand only ~2–3 mm high (Rahman et al. 2015, open full text). Hall et al. 2015 give a wider size range ([mem], about 3 to 40 mm).
- **Shape for an illustrator**: a low circular dome or disc with **three-fold rotational symmetry**, found in no living animal. Three raised ridges ("arms") start at the centre and run outwards, each bending the same way so that near the edge it runs parallel to the rim, like a three-armed pinwheel or triskelion. Rahman et al. describe the arms as curving counterclockwise toward the margin; because fossils are impressions on the underside of sandstone beds, **check the handedness against the figures in Rahman et al. 2015 (open access) before drawing**. Between the arms the surface carries bundles of fine branching grooves fanning to the rim; three small pits sit near the centre. It rested on the mat-covered sea floor, flat side down, unattached or lightly stuck. True height in life is uncertain; the fossils may be somewhat flattened.
- **What it is thought to be**: unknown affinity. Placed in an extinct three-fold group (Trilobozoa or "triradialomorphs"), "of a coelenterate level of organization" (Ivantsov & Zakrevskaya 2021 [abs]); an old comparison with echinoderms is abandoned ([mem]). Security: low for relationships, good for shape. Fluid-flow modelling suggests it fed passively on suspended particles that its ridges steered into the central pits (Rahman et al. 2015 [abs]); Ivantsov & Zakrevskaya 2021 [abs] add that it may have moved slowly.
- **Sources**: Rahman IA, Darroch SAF, Racicot RA, Laflamme M (2015). Suspension feeding in the enigmatic Ediacaran organism *Tribrachidium* demonstrates complexity of Neoproterozoic ecosystems. *Science Advances* 1(10): e1500800. doi:10.1126/sciadv.1500800. — Hall CMS, Droser ML, Gehling JG, Dzaugis ME (2015). Paleoecology of the enigmatic *Tribrachidium*: new data from the Ediacaran of South Australia. *Precambrian Research* 269: 183–194. doi:10.1016/j.precamres.2015.08.009. — Ivantsov AY, Zakrevskaya MA (2021). Trilobozoa, Precambrian tri-radial organisms. *Paleontological Journal* 55(7): 727–741. doi:10.1134/S0031030121070066.

### 3. *Fractofusus* (*F. misrai* and *F. andersoni*)

- **Age and assemblage**: Avalon assemblage, ~575–560 Ma (Gehling & Narbonne 2007 [abs]); abundant on the Mistaken Point "E" surface, 565.00 ± 0.64 Ma (date from Matthews et al. 2021 [abs]; abundance there [mem]).
- **Localities**: only south-eastern Newfoundland, Canada: "endemic to the Newfoundland portion of the Avalonia terrane" (Gehling & Narbonne 2007 [abs]). Mistaken Point on the Avalon Peninsula; also the Bonavista Peninsula ([mem]; Hofmann et al. 2008, title verified).
- **Size**: **[mem], not confirmed this session; check Gehling & Narbonne 2007 before drawing.** *F. misrai* is long and narrow, commonly 10–20 cm and up to about 40 cm long; *F. andersoni* is shorter and more oval, mostly under 15 cm.
- **Shape for an illustrator**: a flat spindle, widest in the middle and tapering to a blunt point at each end, with no stalk, no disc and no head or tail. A zigzag line runs down the middle. From it, two rows of leaf-like units ("frondlets") spread outwards, one row each side, alternating left and right, not paired. Each frondlet is a miniature of the whole pattern, subdivided into smaller copies of itself, at least three levels deep (rangeomorph branching; Narbonne 2004 [abs]). Frondlets are largest mid-body and shrink toward both tips. It **lay flat on the sea floor**, with one face on the mud; unlike *Charnia* it did not stand up, and fossils are never bent over by currents ([mem]). Show it as a low quilted mat, never as an upright frond.
- **What it is thought to be**: a rangeomorph. "Biological affinities remain uncertain", with a position "near the base of the Metazoa" suggested (Gehling & Narbonne 2007 [abs]); the related *Charnia* is argued to be a stem-group eumetazoan animal (Dunn et al. 2021 [abs]). Security: moderate that it is an animal-grade organism, low on anything closer. It was not a plant or alga: it lived below the sunlit zone. Spatial patterns show it reproduced both by runners (stolons), giving clusters of small individuals around large ones, and by waterborne propagules (Mitchell et al. 2015 [abs]); a cluster of sizes is therefore accurate.
- **Sources**: Gehling JG, Narbonne GM (2007). Spindle-shaped Ediacara fossils from the Mistaken Point assemblage, Avalon Zone, Newfoundland. *Canadian Journal of Earth Sciences* 44(3): 367–387. doi:10.1139/E07-003. — Mitchell EG, Kenchington CG, Liu AG, Matthews JJ, Butterfield NJ (2015). Reconstructing the reproductive mode of an Ediacaran macro-organism. *Nature* 524(7565): 343–346. doi:10.1038/nature14646. — Hofmann HJ, O'Brien SJ, King AF (2008). Ediacaran biota on Bonavista Peninsula, Newfoundland, Canada. *Journal of Paleontology* 82(1): 1–36. doi:10.1666/06-087.1.

### 4. *Cloudina*

- **Age and assemblage**: Nama assemblage, terminal Ediacaran, ~550–539 Ma (Liang et al. 2020 [abs]); Namibian examples ~548 Ma (Penny et al. 2014 [abs]). Last in-place occurrences precede the first Cambrian-type burrows (Bowyer et al. 2022 [abs]).
- **Localities**: Nama Group, Namibia (type area; Germs 1972); Dengying Formation, South China (Hua et al. 2005 [abs]; Liang et al. 2020 [abs]); "globally distributed" (Penny et al. 2014 [abs]). Closely related tubes ("cloudinomorphs") occur in Nevada, USA (Schiffbauer et al. 2020 [abs]). Other countries commonly listed (Oman, Spain, Brazil, Siberia) are [mem].
- **Size**: small. **[mem], not confirmed this session**: tubes about 0.3 to 6.5 mm wide and up to about 3.5 cm long, depending on species (Germs 1972). Confirmed for the related Nevada tubes: median width about 0.7–1.1 mm, largest about 4–6 mm (Schiffbauer et al. 2020, open full text). Tube walls are very thin ([mem], tens of micrometres). Tubes from wave-swept reefs are the widest and those from quiet mat-covered floors are smaller (Shore & Wood 2021, open full text).
- **Shape for an illustrator**: a slender tube, nearly straight to gently curving (measured tubes in a Namibian reef are on average only 4% longer than the straight line between their ends; Shore & Wood 2021, open full text), made of a stack of thin-walled funnels, each sitting inside the one below, "eccentrically and sometimes deeply nested" and with no floors (cross-walls) between them (Hua et al. 2005 [abs]). The flared rims of successive funnels project on the outside, giving a ringed, collared look like a stack of paper cups pushed together slightly off-centre. Cross-section round to oval; the base is closed and bulbous, with no root or holdfast; the top is open (features listed in Schiffbauer et al. 2020, full text). Some tubes fork into two (Hua et al. 2005 [abs]). The wall was calcium carbonate, weakly mineralised. Some tubes carry small round holes, 40–400 micrometres across, read as the oldest predator drill-holes (Bengtson & Zhao 1992 [abs]). It lived on shallow carbonate sea floors among microbial mounds; whether tubes stood upright cemented to one another as reefs (Penny et al. 2014 [abs]) or lay loose and were swept into heaps (Mehra & Maloof 2018 [abs]) is disputed. **The soft animal is unknown**: any tentacles or body shown emerging from the tube are CONVENTION.
- **What it is thought to be**: an animal, "the earliest known skeletal metazoan" (Penny et al. 2014 [abs]); that much is secure. Which animal is not: tube structure and budding are "broadly similar to modern serpulid annelids", by relationship or by convergence (Hua et al. 2005 [abs]); a preserved gut in related Nevada tubes suggests a bilaterian, worm-like builder (Schiffbauer et al. 2020 [abs], who note "alternative interpretations are plausible"); a cnidarian (coral-like) identity has also been argued (discussed in Schiffbauer et al. 2020, full text).
- **Sources**: Germs GJB (1972). New shelly fossils from Nama Group, South West Africa. *American Journal of Science* 272(8): 752–761. doi:10.2475/ajs.272.8.752. — Hua H, Chen Z, Yuan X, Zhang L, Xiao S (2005). Skeletogenesis and asexual reproduction in the earliest biomineralizing animal *Cloudina*. *Geology* 33(4): 277–280. doi:10.1130/G21198.1. — Schiffbauer JD, Selly T, Jacquet SM, Merz RA, Nelson LL, Strange MA, Cai Y, Smith EF (2020). Discovery of bilaterian-type through-guts in cloudinomorphs from the terminal Ediacaran Period. *Nature Communications* 11: 205. doi:10.1038/s41467-019-13882-z. — Penny et al. 2014 (under event 9). — Mehra A, Maloof A (2018). Multiscale approach reveals that *Cloudina* aggregates are detritus and not in situ reef constructions. *PNAS* 115(11). doi:10.1073/pnas.1719911115 (Crossref gives no pages; E2519–E2527 is [mem]). — Bengtson S, Zhao Y (1992). Predatorial borings in late Precambrian mineralized exoskeletons. *Science* 257(5068): 367–369. doi:10.1126/science.257.5068.367. — Shore A, Wood R (2021). Environmental and diagenetic controls on the morphology and calcification of the Ediacaran metazoan *Cloudina*. *Scientific Reports* 11: 12341. doi:10.1038/s41598-021-90768-5. — Liang D, Cai Y, Nolan M, Xiao S (2020). The terminal Ediacaran tubular fossil *Cloudina* in the Yangtze Gorges area of South China. *Precambrian Research* 351: 105931. doi:10.1016/j.precamres.2020.105931.

### Considered and not chosen

No sources were checked for these; the notes are from memory and are only reasons for the choice.
- *Spriggina* (White Sea assemblage): the best reserve. Segmented, bilateral-looking, with a crescent "head"; visually striking but its identity is strongly disputed and it would be a third White Sea organism.
- *Parvancorina*, *Funisia* (White Sea): more White Sea forms; less distinctive in silhouette than *Tribrachidium*.
- *Charniodiscus*/*Arborea*: fronds, too like *Charnia* in a small picture.
- *Rangea*, *Pteridinium* (Nama): good Nama candidates, but three-dimensional and hard to reconstruct; *Cloudina* is the clearer choice because it marks the first skeletons.
