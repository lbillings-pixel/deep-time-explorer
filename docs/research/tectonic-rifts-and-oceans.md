# Dossier: continental rifts and the opening of oceans and seas

Research notes behind the site's texts and pictures, not text written for visitors, except the two "sentences for visitors" in each entry. Compiled 14 November 2026.

## How to read this

- **Citations.** Every citation was looked up in the Crossref database (authors, year, title, journal, volume, pages, DOI) on 14 November 2026. Anything not found there is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Author lists longer than eight are shortened to the first three plus "et al." with the author count.
- **Facts.** Each fact carries a tag:
  - **[abstract]** read in the paper's own abstract. The abstract text came from Crossref or, where Crossref holds none, from the OpenAlex index of the same DOI. Some abstracts were cut off at about 1,500 to 1,800 characters by my script, so a date that sits late in a long abstract may have been missed.
  - **[title]** stated in the paper's title only (the abstract was not available).
  - **[full text]** read in open-access full text. *No fact in this dossier has this tag: I read no full texts.*
  - **[secondary]** seen only in a citing paper, search summary or encyclopedia.
  - **[memory]** widely repeated, but not confirmed in a source during this work. **Check before use.** Many extents on land and many "what is at the surface" notes are [memory].
- **Papers cited without an abstract read.** Where a paper is listed as a key source but neither Crossref nor OpenAlex gave its abstract, the entry says "citation checked; abstract not read". Such a paper supports nothing in this dossier by itself; it is listed because it is the standard review to check against.
- **Time scale.** Period and epoch names follow the International Chronostratigraphic Chart (Cohen et al. 2013, updated; *Episodes* 36: 199-204, doi:10.18814/epiiugs/2013/v36i3/002, as cited in the project's other dossiers; I did not re-read the chart in this work, so boundary ages quoted from it are [memory]).
- **Kinds.** *rift that failed*: the continent stretched but did not break. *rift still active*. *ocean opening*: the rift went on to make sea floor. *back-arc sea opening*: a sea opened behind a subduction zone.
- **Dates for an ocean opening.** Three are given where known: when rifting began, when the first sea floor formed ("breakup"), and when the sea stopped widening, if it did. "App start / end" is my recommendation for the two numbers in `events.ts`. My rule: start = onset of rifting where a source dates it, otherwise the first sea floor; end = the first sea floor along the last segment to break, or, for small seas that later stopped widening, the end of spreading. Each entry says which was used.
- **Boxes** are `[south, west, north, east]` in decimal degrees, drawn by me from general knowledge of where the basins and margins lie, not traced from a map. All boxes are **[memory]** and generous by about half a degree. They need a visual check on a map before use.
- **Provinces** are from Hasterok et al. 2022 (citation checked: Hasterok, D., Halpin, J.A., Collins, A.S., Hand, M., Kreemer, C., Gard, M.G. & Glorie, S. 2022. New maps of global geological provinces and tectonic plates. *Earth-Science Reviews* 231: 104069. doi:10.1016/j.earscirev.2022.104069), by id and name as in the project's table of 921 provinces. I matched provinces by name, type and bounding box only; I did not see their outlines. Note that many "passive margin" provinces in that map are mostly offshore, so a visitor's point on land will often fall outside them.

**On the total.** The brief asked for 16 to 20. I give 22 because the Rio Grande Rift and the Gulf of California each have their own province in the Hasterok map and their own well-dated history, and merging them into the Basin and Range would blur three different stories. If the list must be cut to 20, drop **5** (dates not sourced in this work) and **9** (almost nothing on land), or hold them back until checked. **Coverage is uneven.** South America has only one entry (7), against the brief's wish for several per continent: the continent's other rifts (the Amazonas graben, the Cretaceous rifts of north-east Brazil, the Salado and Colorado basins) are either buried or part of entry 7. See "Considered and left out" at the end for Rheic, Neo-Tethys, Drake Passage and the Tasmanian Gateway.

**A structural point for whoever writes the code.** The existing `MajorEvent` type requires `rock: 'volcanic'` and uses an event only to date loosely dated lava at the point. Most rift events have no such rock test: the evidence is a basin, a fault scarp or a coastline. These entries therefore need either a new field (for example a list of province ids, or a rock test of "rift-basin sediment") or to be shown as regional context only. The boxes alone are too generous to assert anything about a single point.

## 1. Midcontinent Rift

1. **Other names:** Keweenawan Rift; North American Midcontinent Rift System.
2. **Kind:** rift that failed.
3. **Ages.** Volcanism between about 1109 and 1083 Ma (Swanson-Hysell et al. 2019) [abstract]. The youngest dated lavas are a tuff at 1084.35 ± 0.20 Ma and a rhyolite at 1083.52 ± 0.23 Ma on Michipicoten Island, Ontario; rift magmatism "was active for at least 25 m.y." (Fairchild et al. 2017) [abstract]. Sediment went on filling the sagging basin after the lavas stopped [memory]. **App: 1109 to 1083**, the span of dated rift lavas (U-Pb zircon). No real disagreement on these dates.
4. **What pulled apart:** the interior of Laurentia, the old core of North America, began to split. It stopped before an ocean formed [title of Swanson-Hysell et al. 2019: "Failed rifting"].
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes, all [memory]: Lake Superior shores `[46.0, -92.5, 49.2, -84.0]`; western arm under Minnesota, Wisconsin and Iowa `[41.5, -96.5, 46.5, -91.5]`; its continuation under Nebraska and Kansas `[38.5, -98.0, 41.5, -95.5]`; eastern arm under Michigan `[41.5, -86.5, 46.5, -83.0]`. The rift rocks are at the surface only around Lake Superior; the arms are buried under younger sedimentary rock and known from gravity and magnetic surveys and drilling [memory]. (b) Province **568 Mid-continent Rift** (narrow rift; last orogeny "failed rift"). A good match; use the province in preference to the boxes.
6. **At the surface today:** dark basalt cliffs and headlands on the north shore of Lake Superior, the Keweenaw Peninsula and Isle Royale; the native copper of Michigan; red sandstones around the lake [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "About 1.1 billion years ago North America began to split in two along a great curved rift through what is now Lake Superior. Lava poured out for about 25 million years, then the rifting stopped, and the continent stayed whole."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Swanson-Hysell, N.L., Ramezani, J., Fairchild, L.M. & Rose, I.R. 2019. Failed rifting and fast drifting: Midcontinent Rift development, Laurentia's rapid motion and the driver of Grenvillian orogenesis. *GSA Bulletin* 131(5-6): 913-940. doi:10.1130/b31944.1
   - Fairchild, L.M., Swanson-Hysell, N.L., Ramezani, J., Sprain, C.J. & Bowring, S.A. 2017. The end of Midcontinent Rift magmatism and the paleogeography of Laurentia. *Lithosphere* 9(1): 117-133. doi:10.1130/l580.1
   - Stein, C.A., Kley, J., Stein, S., Hindle, D. & Keller, G.R. 2015. North America's Midcontinent Rift: when rift met LIP. *Geosphere* 11: 1607-1616. doi:10.1130/ges01183.1 (citation checked; abstract not read).
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "Away from Lake Superior the rift is buried under younger rock and is known from drilling and from measurements of gravity and magnetism." Why the rift failed is debated; the first source ties it to mountain building on the continent's edge, but I read that only in its title.

## 2. Opening of the Iapetus Ocean (Laurentian margin)

1. **Other names:** breakup of Rodinia (final stage); Iapetan rifting; "Neo-Iapetus" in Robert et al. 2020.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening. The ocean later closed completely (the Caledonian and Appalachian mountain building), so only its rifted edge survives.
3. **Ages.** Two phases of rifting are recorded on the margins: 750 to 680 Ma and 615 to 550 Ma (Robert et al. 2020) [abstract]. Conventionally the second phase opened the ocean and the first failed; Robert et al. instead propose two oceans, a "Paleo-Iapetus" from about 700 Ma and a "Neo-Iapetus" from 600 Ma [abstract]. Cawood et al. 2001: Laurentia separated from the west Gondwana cratons about 570 Ma, with a wide ocean by 550 Ma; in Newfoundland the youngest rift lavas and intrusions are 550.5 +3/−2 Ma and 555 +3/−5 Ma (U-Pb zircon), the change from rifting to drifting is about 540 to 535 Ma, and the oldest sediments of the settled margin are about 525 to 520 Ma [abstract]. **App: 615 to 535** (start of the second rift phase to the rift-to-drift change in Newfoundland). A narrower alternative is 600 to 550. **Disagreement:** whether one ocean or two; whether breakup was about 570 or about 540 Ma (Cawood et al. reconcile these as two stages).
4. **What pulled apart:** Laurentia (ancient North America) from Baltica and Amazonia (Robert et al. 2020) [abstract].
5. **Extent on land. Is there an honest region?** Yes, but only on the North American side and only as a narrow strip: the old edge of Laurentia now caught up along the western side of the Appalachians. Rift lavas and dykes of this age are reported from western Newfoundland (Skinner Cove Formation, Lady Slipper pluton) [abstract]; and, from memory, the Long Range dykes of Newfoundland and Labrador, the Tibbit Hill and related volcanic rocks of southern Quebec and Vermont, and the Catoctin Formation of the Blue Ridge in Virginia and Maryland [memory]. (a) Boxes [memory]: western Newfoundland `[47.5, -59.8, 51.8, -56.3]`; southern Quebec and Vermont `[43.5, -74.0, 47.5, -70.5]`; central Appalachian Blue Ridge `[35.0, -84.0, 40.0, -77.0]`. Do not extend to Europe: the Scottish and Scandinavian pieces are thrust sheets moved far from where they formed [memory]. (b) Province **647 Laurentides** (passive margin, Appalachians group) is the nearest match; it runs from Alabama to Newfoundland (box 32.9 to 51.8 N). Province 644 "Granderia" is also typed "passive margin" but I take it to be Ganderia, a block that arrived from the far side of the ocean; do **not** use it. Honest answer: the map's province is broader than the evidence.
6. **At the surface today:** green-black altered lava (the Catoctin greenstone) along Skyline Drive and the Blue Ridge Parkway; dark dykes cutting old gneiss in Newfoundland and Labrador [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "Between about 615 and 535 million years ago an older supercontinent broke up here and a vanished ocean, the Iapetus, opened along this edge of ancient North America. The ocean closed again long ago; the Appalachians mark where."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Robert, B., Domeier, M. & Jakob, J. 2020. Iapetan Oceans: an analog of Tethys? *Geology* 48(9): 929-933. doi:10.1130/g47513.1
   - Cawood, P.A., McCausland, P.J.A. & Dunning, G.R. 2001. Opening Iapetus: constraints from the Laurentian margin in Newfoundland. *Geological Society of America Bulletin* 113(4): 443-453. doi:10.1130/0016-7606(2001)113<0443:oicftl>2.0.co;2
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The ocean no longer exists. The date is for the rifting recorded in rocks along the old continental edge; these rocks were later folded into the Appalachians." Low priority; include only if the editor wants a Precambrian entry in eastern North America.

## 3. Central Atlantic, with the Triassic-Jurassic rift basins of eastern North America and Morocco

1. **Other names:** breakup of Pangaea (first stage); Newark Supergroup rift basins; Eastern North American rift system; Atlas rift (the Moroccan arm, later squeezed up into the Atlas Mountains).
2. **Kind:** ocean opening.
3. **Ages.**
   - *Rifting began:* late Ladinian, about 230 Ma, along eastern North America, north-west Africa and the Atlas (Schettino & Turco 2009) [abstract]. This rests on the age of the oldest rift-basin sediments.
   - *Lava:* the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province peaked at about 200 Ma [abstract, Marzoli et al. 1999] and is tied to the end-Triassic extinction at about 201 Ma (Blackburn et al. 2013) [abstract]; intrusions began about 100,000 years before the oldest known flows (Davies et al. 2017) [abstract]. It covered more than 7 million km² across North America, Europe, Africa and South America (Marzoli et al. 1999) [abstract].
   - *First sea floor (breakup):* **disputed.** Schettino & Turco 2009: sea floor from 200 Ma between the East Coast and Blake Spur magnetic anomalies, with the main plate boundary jumping at 185 Ma [abstract]. Withjack et al. 1998: the change from rifting to drifting came earlier in the south (before the lavas at about 200 Ma in the south-eastern United States) than in the north (before about 185 Ma off Maritime Canada) [abstract, read through an automated summary of the Crossref record]. Labails et al. 2010 argue for an early, slow opening from about 190 Ma [memory; citation checked, abstract not read]. Older literature put the first sea floor near 175 Ma [memory].
   - **App: 230 to 185.** Start = oldest rift sediments; end = the latest of the breakup estimates that I read in an abstract (off Maritime Canada). The lava at about 201 Ma would make a good separate entry in the existing lava list, since it is the one rift rock here that can date loosely dated basalt.
4. **What pulled apart:** North America from north-west Africa, splitting the supercontinent Pangaea.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: Fundy basin, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick `[44.0, -67.0, 46.0, -63.0]`; Connecticut Valley (Hartford and Deerfield basins) `[41.0, -73.3, 42.9, -72.0]`; Newark, Gettysburg and Culpeper basins, New Jersey to Virginia `[38.0, -78.3, 41.4, -73.8]`; Richmond, Dan River and Deep River basins, Virginia and North Carolina `[35.0, -80.6, 38.0, -77.0]`; buried South Georgia rift under the coastal plain `[30.8, -85.5, 33.6, -80.5]`; Morocco, Argana valley, High Atlas and coastal Meseta `[29.5, -10.5, 34.5, -4.0]`. Inland limit: the exposed basins lie 50 to 300 km from today's coast; beyond the westernmost basin edge nothing is defensible. Farther south in Africa (Mauritania, Senegal) the rift is under younger coastal-plain sediment [memory]. (b) Provinces: **670 Meguma Terrane** (passive margin; box 33.9 to 45.7 N, 76.3 to 56.0 W, which looks like the offshore margin from the Carolinas to Nova Scotia), **669 Blake Plateau**, **653 Bahama Platform**, **39 West African Rifted Margin** (3.9 to 34.8 N). **The map has no province for the exposed rift basins of the eastern United States or for the Moroccan rift:** they sit inside Appalachian and Atlas (id 12) orogenic provinces. For those, the boxes are all we have.
6. **At the surface today:** red sandstone and shale valleys with dinosaur footprints (Connecticut Valley, Newark basin); dark basalt and dolerite ridges, including the Palisades on the Hudson, the Watchung Mountains and the Holyoke Range; the red cliffs and basalt of the Bay of Fundy; red beds and basalt in the Argana valley and High Atlas of Morocco [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "From about 230 million years ago the supercontinent Pangaea began to tear apart here, opening long valleys that filled with red mud and sand and, about 201 million years ago, with floods of lava. The tear became the Atlantic Ocean, whose first sea floor formed between about 200 and 185 million years ago."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Schettino, A. & Turco, E. 2009. Breakup of Pangaea and plate kinematics of the central Atlantic and Atlas regions. *Geophysical Journal International* 178(2): 1078-1097. doi:10.1111/j.1365-246x.2009.04186.x
   - Marzoli, A., Renne, P.R., Piccirillo, E.M., Ernesto, M., Bellieni, G. & De Min, A. 1999. Extensive 200-million-year-old continental flood basalts of the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province. *Science* 284(5414): 616-618. doi:10.1126/science.284.5414.616
   - Blackburn, T.J., Olsen, P.E., Bowring, S.A., et al. [9 authors] 2013. Zircon U-Pb geochronology links the end-Triassic extinction with the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province. *Science* 340(6135): 941-945. doi:10.1126/science.1234204
   - Withjack, M.O., Schlische, R.W., Olsen, P.E. & Zhang, Q. 1998. Diachronous rifting, drifting, and inversion on the passive margin of central eastern North America: an analog for other passive margins. *AAPG Bulletin* 82: 817-835. doi:10.1306/1d9bc60b-172d-11d7-8645000102c1865d
   - Also checked: Labails, C., Olivet, J.-L., Aslanian, D. & Roest, W.R. 2010. An alternative early opening scenario for the Central Atlantic Ocean. *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 297(3-4): 355-368. doi:10.1016/j.epsl.2010.06.024 (abstract not read). Davies, J.H.F.L., Marzoli, A., Bertrand, H., Youbi, N., Ernesto, M. & Schaltegger, U. 2017. *Nature Communications* 8: 15596. doi:10.1038/ncomms15596. Withjack, M.O., Schlische, R.W., Olsen, P.E. & Kinney, S.T. 2024. The rifted margin of eastern North America: insights into rifting, igneous activity, and breakup. In *Regional Geology and Tectonics*: 53-83. doi:10.1016/b978-0-444-64136-6.00001-4 (the most recent review; abstract not read).
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The rifting took tens of millions of years, and the ocean seems to have opened earlier in the south than in the north. Estimates of the first sea floor range from about 200 to about 175 million years ago."

## 4. Gulf of Mexico

1. **Other names:** none in common use.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening that then stopped. Stern & Dickinson 2010 class it as a **back-arc basin**, opened behind a volcanic arc in Mexico [abstract]; most authors treat it as an arm of the Pangaea breakup [memory]. Report both.
3. **Ages.**
   - *Rifting:* Triassic to Middle Jurassic stretching, approaching 100% in eastern Mexico (Pindell et al. 2020) [abstract]. No single start date in the abstracts I read; Late Triassic, roughly 230 to 200 Ma, is the usual statement [memory].
   - *Salt:* the great salt layer (Louann Salt) was laid down late in the stretching and just before sea-floor spreading (Pulham et al. 2019) [abstract]. Its age was long taken as Callovian [abstract, Pulham et al.], about 165 to 161 Ma on the ICS chart [memory]; strontium isotopes now give Bajocian (Pindell et al. 2020) [abstract], about 170 to 168 Ma [memory].
   - *Sea floor:* spreading about 165 to 142 Ma (Stern & Dickinson 2010) [abstract]. Extinct spreading ridges are visible in satellite gravity under the Gulf's sediments (Sandwell et al. 2014) [abstract]. With the older salt, spreading may have begun a few million years earlier than 165 Ma; I did not read a revised figure.
   - **App: 165 to 142** (span of sea-floor spreading, the only pair I read in an abstract). Rests on plate-model fitting, not on dated magnetic stripes, which are weak in the Gulf [memory].
4. **What pulled apart:** the Yucatán block rotated away from the southern edge of North America (Texas, Louisiana, Florida) [memory for the rotation; the opening pole in Stern & Dickinson's abstract lies near Florida, consistent with it].
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: United States Gulf coastal plain including the interior salt basins of east Texas, north Louisiana and Mississippi `[27.5, -98.5, 33.5, -84.5]`; Mexican Gulf coastal plain, Tamaulipas to Veracruz `[18.0, -99.0, 27.5, -96.3]`; Yucatán peninsula `[17.5, -92.5, 21.8, -86.5]`; Florida's west side `[25.0, -84.0, 30.5, -81.5]`. Inland limit: the landward edge of the salt basins, 300 to 400 km from the coast in Texas and Louisiana. Everything rift-related is deeply buried. (b) Provinces: **672 Northern Gulf of Mexico Margin**, **673 Suwannee Terrane**, **583 Maya Terrane** (all "passive margin"). Reasonable matches.
6. **At the surface today:** nothing of the rift itself. A flat coastal plain; low hills pushed up by rising salt (Avery Island and the other "Five Islands" of Louisiana) and the salt mines beneath them [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "The Gulf of Mexico opened in the Jurassic, when a block of crust that is now the Yucatán swung away from the rest of North America. New sea floor formed from about 165 to about 142 million years ago; then the Gulf stopped widening."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Pindell, J., Villagómez, D., Molina-Garza, R., Graham, R. & Weber, B. 2020. A revised synthesis of the rift and drift history of the Gulf of Mexico and surrounding regions in the light of improved age dating of the Middle Jurassic salt. *Geological Society, London, Special Publications* 504(1): 29-76. doi:10.1144/sp504-2020-43
   - Stern, R.J. & Dickinson, W.R. 2010. The Gulf of Mexico is a Jurassic backarc basin. *Geosphere* 6(6): 739-754. doi:10.1130/ges00585.1
   - Pulham, A.J., Peel, F.J., Rives, T., Delph, B., Salel, J.-F., Nicholson, T., Wu, J. & Requejo, R. 2019. The age of the Louann Salt; insights from historic isotopic analyses in salt stocks from the onshore interior salt basins of the northern Gulf of Mexico. In *Salt Tectonics, Associated Processes, and Exploration Potential: Revisited 1989-2019* (GCSSEPM): 64-66. doi:10.5724/gcs.37.064
   - Sandwell, D.T., Müller, R.D., Smith, W.H.F., Garcia, E. & Francis, R. 2014. New global marine gravity model from CryoSat-2 and Jason-1 reveals buried tectonic structure. *Science* 346(6205): 65-67. doi:10.1126/science.1258213
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The evidence is buried under kilometres of younger sediment and comes from drilling and geophysical surveys. The dates come from fitting the plates back together and could shift by several million years."

## 5. Africa parts from Madagascar, India and Antarctica (Somali and Mozambique basins)

1. **Other names:** breakup of East and West Gondwana; opening of the Western Somali Basin, the Mozambique Basin and the Riiser-Larsen Sea.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening. Spreading in the Somali Basin later stopped.
3. **Ages. This entry is the least well sourced: none of the key dating papers gave an abstract through Crossref or OpenAlex.**
   - *Rifting and lava:* the Karoo flood basalts, about 183 Ma, already in `events.ts` [project file]. Rift basins along this margin are older still, from the Permian [title of Macgregor 2017, "Permian-Cretaceous rifts in East Africa"; that paper was seen only as a Crossref listing].
   - *First sea floor:* Middle to Late Jurassic. Figures I recall: a breakup unconformity in western Madagascar in the Middle Jurassic (Geiger et al. 2004); oldest magnetic stripes of about 155 to 165 Ma in the Mozambique and Somali basins (Leinweber & Jokat 2012; Mueller & Jokat 2019; Gaina et al. 2013) **[memory, all]**.
   - *Stopped:* Phethean et al. 2016 find the Western Somali Basin symmetric about magnetic stripe M0 and map its extinct ridge [abstract]; M0 is about 121 Ma [memory; Gibbons et al. 2013 give "M0 (~120.4 Ma)" in their abstract]. Madagascar has not moved relative to Africa since.
   - **App: 183 to 155**, start = Karoo lavas, end = a cautious figure for established sea floor. **Check both against Mueller & Jokat 2019 before use.**
4. **What pulled apart:** Africa (West Gondwana, with South America still attached) from a block made of Madagascar, India, Antarctica and Australia (East Gondwana). Madagascar slid south along the East African coast (Phethean et al. 2016) [abstract].
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: Lebombo range, South Africa, Eswatini and Mozambique `[-27.5, 31.4, -22.0, 32.7]`; Mozambique coastal plain `[-26.5, 32.5, -15.0, 41.0]`; coastal Tanzania and Kenya `[-11.5, 38.0, -1.5, 41.5]`; coastal Somalia `[-1.5, 40.5, 11.5, 51.5]`; western Madagascar (Morondava and Mahajanga basins) `[-25.5, 43.0, -12.0, 47.5]`. Inland limit: the edge of the coastal sedimentary basins, 100 to 300 km. Phethean et al. conclude that offshore Tanzania is an obliquely rifted margin and that the true sliding margin lies along the Rovuma Basin [abstract], which supports including the Tanzanian coast. (b) Provinces: **38 Somali Margin** (passive margin; 7.6 S to 12.9 N); **44 South African Rifted Margin** (box 37.4 to 12.8 S, 10.8 to 32.3 E, so it wraps the whole southern tip and mixes this event with the South Atlantic: split it by longitude, east of about 25 E for this event). **No province for western Madagascar's basins or the Mozambique coastal plain** was found under a rift or margin type. Antarctic side: **882 Antarctic Rifted Margin** (9.7 to 35.0 E), uninhabited.
6. **At the surface today:** the long straight Lebombo ridge of tilted lava on the Mozambique border; the wide coastal plain of Mozambique; the limestone "tsingy" and sandstone country of western Madagascar, which are sediments laid down on the new margin [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "In the Jurassic the southern supercontinent Gondwana split in two along this coast. Madagascar, India and Antarctica together moved away from Africa, and new sea floor formed between them from roughly 165 to 155 million years ago." (Use "in the Jurassic" alone if the dates cannot be checked.)
8. **Key sources** (all citations checked; **abstracts not read except Phethean**).

## 6. India leaves Antarctica and Australia

1. **Other names:** breakup of East Gondwana; opening of the Enderby Basin, the Bay of Bengal and the Perth Abyssal Plain.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening.
3. **Ages.**
   - *First sea floor:* the spreading ridge that separated Greater India from Australia and Antarctica grew from north to south, starting about 136 Ma north-west of Australia and reaching the southern tip of India about 126 Ma (Gibbons et al. 2013) [abstract]. Off Antarctica, magnetic stripes run from M9o (about 130.2 Ma) to M2o (about 124.1 Ma); the breakups of India from Antarctica and India from Australia were "roughly contemporaneous" (Gaina et al. 2007) [abstract].
   - *Rifting began:* not dated in any abstract I read. The rift valleys of eastern India (Mahanadi, Godavari) and the Perth Basin hold sediments from the Permian onward, so stretching long pre-dates breakup [memory].
   - *Later:* a ridge jump at about 115 Ma left a sliver of continent (Elan Bank) on the Antarctic plate (Gibbons et al. 2013) [abstract].
   - **App: 136 to 126** (first sea floor, north to south; rests on magnetic stripes). The start is a breakup date, not a rift date, because no rift-onset date was sourced.
4. **What pulled apart:** India (then much larger, "Greater India") from Antarctica on its south-east side and from the west coast of Australia.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: Western Australia, Perth Basin `[-35.0, 114.5, -27.0, 116.6]` and Carnarvon coast `[-27.0, 112.8, -20.0, 116.0]`; India, Cauvery basin `[8.0, 77.5, 12.5, 80.5]`, Krishna-Godavari coast `[14.5, 79.5, 18.5, 83.0]`, Mahanadi coast and Bengal `[19.0, 84.0, 23.5, 88.8]`; eastern Sri Lanka `[5.5, 80.5, 9.9, 82.0]`. Inland limit: 100 to 150 km, the width of the coastal basins; the Darling Fault east of Perth is the sharp inland edge in Australia [memory]. (b) Provinces: **795 East Indian Margin**, **555 Sri Lanka Margin**, **Western Australian Rifted Margin** (the table gives id 406 to this and to four other rows: Batavia Knoll, Gulden Draak, Zenith Plateau, Quokka Rise; check the id), **404 Exmouth Plateau**, **881 Antarctic Rifted Margin** (32.0 to 95.8 E), **103 Elan Bank**. Provinces **538 Mahanadi Rift** and **550 Godavari Rift** ("failed rift") are the older Permian-Triassic valleys; they run into this margin but are not the same event, so list them only if the tile says so.
6. **At the surface today:** the Darling Scarp behind Perth; the flat Swan Coastal Plain; the broad deltas of the Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi and Kaveri, which sit in basins that sagged as the margin formed [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "India once lay against Antarctica and the west coast of Australia. It broke away between about 136 and 126 million years ago, the split running from north to south, and the eastern Indian Ocean opened behind it."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Gibbons, A.D., Whittaker, J.M. & Müller, R.D. 2013. The breakup of East Gondwana: assimilating constraints from Cretaceous ocean basins around India into a best-fit tectonic model. *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* 118(3): 808-822. doi:10.1002/jgrb.50079
   - Gaina, C., Müller, R.D., Brown, B., Ishihara, T. & Ivanov, S. 2007. Breakup and early seafloor spreading between India and Antarctica. *Geophysical Journal International* 170(1): 151-169. doi:10.1111/j.1365-246x.2007.03450.x
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The date is for the first sea floor. The land along these coasts had been stretching and sinking, on and off, for more than a hundred million years before that."

## 7. South Atlantic

1. **Other names:** breakup of West Gondwana; South Atlantic rift.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening.
3. **Ages, by segment.**
   - *Rifting:* slow east-west stretching from 140 Ma to about 126 Ma, then faster and in a new direction (Heine et al. 2013) [abstract]. Lava: the Paraná-Etendeka flood basalts, about 135 to 132 Ma, already in `events.ts`.
   - *First sea floor, far south:* 138 Ma (Pérez-Díaz & Eagles 2014, from sea-floor spreading data) [abstract].
   - *Central segment (the salt basins of Brazil, Angola, Congo and Gabon):* a single salt basin was split at about 112 Ma, the Aptian-Albian boundary, when spreading began north of the Walvis Ridge (Torsvik et al. 2009) [abstract]; final breakup of the Santos-Benguela segment at about 113 Ma (Heine et al. 2013) [abstract].
   - *Equatorial Atlantic (north Brazil to Ghana):* 103 Ma (Heine et al. 2013) [abstract]; Africa was a single rigid plate again by 106 Ma (Pérez-Díaz & Eagles 2014) [abstract].
   - **App: 140 to 112** for the South Atlantic proper. If the equatorial coasts (north-east Brazil, Gulf of Guinea) are included in the region, use 103 as the end. **Disagreement:** Moulin et al. 2010 and Aslanian & Moulin 2010 (a published comment on Torsvik et al.) dispute the fit and the amount of deformation inside the continents; I did not read their abstracts.
4. **What pulled apart:** South America from Africa, unzipping from south to north.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]. South America: Argentine and Uruguayan margin with the Salado and Colorado basins `[-41.0, -63.0, -33.0, -53.0]`; south-east Brazil behind the Santos, Campos and Espírito Santo basins `[-29.5, -49.5, -17.5, -39.0]`; the Recôncavo-Tucano-Jatobá rift and the Sergipe-Alagoas coast `[-13.5, -39.5, -8.0, -34.5]`; equatorial Brazil, Potiguar to Ceará `[-6.5, -41.0, -2.5, -35.0]`. Africa: Namibia and western South Africa `[-34.5, 11.5, -17.0, 19.0]`; Angola, Congo and Gabon coastal basins `[-17.0, 8.5, 1.0, 14.5]`; Gulf of Guinea coast, Côte d'Ivoire to Nigeria `[3.5, -8.0, 7.5, 9.5]`; Benue Trough, Nigeria, a branch that failed `[6.0, 7.0, 11.0, 13.0]`. Inland limit: 100 to 200 km in general; the Recôncavo-Tucano-Jatobá rift and the Benue Trough reach 400 km or more inland. (b) Provinces: **756 Argentina Volcanic Margin** (47.9 to 20.5 S; appears to include south-east Brazil), **771 Brazil Rifted Margin** (14.2 S to 10.0 N), **755 East Malvinas Basin**, **37 Central African Margin** (13.3 S to 6.8 N), **44 South African Rifted Margin** (the part west of about 25 E). **No province** was found for the Benue Trough or the Recôncavo-Tucano rift. Province 747 "Amazonian Graben" (failed rift) is a much older structure; do not attach it.
6. **At the surface today:** the matching outlines of the two coasts; the steep coastal ranges of south-east Brazil (Serra do Mar) and the Great Escarpment of Namibia and Angola; lava plateaus on both sides (Paraná, Etendeka); the oil-bearing coastal basins of Brazil, Angola and Gabon [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "South America and Africa were one land until the Early Cretaceous. They began to pull apart about 140 million years ago, and the South Atlantic opened like a zip from south to north, with sea floor here by about [138 / 112 / 103, by segment] million years ago."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Heine, C., Zoethout, J. & Müller, R.D. 2013. Kinematics of the South Atlantic rift. *Solid Earth* 4(2): 215-253. doi:10.5194/se-4-215-2013 (open access)
   - Torsvik, T.H., Rousse, S., Labails, C. & Smethurst, M.A. 2009. A new scheme for the opening of the South Atlantic Ocean and the dissection of an Aptian salt basin. *Geophysical Journal International* 177(3): 1315-1333. doi:10.1111/j.1365-246x.2009.04137.x
   - Pérez-Díaz, L. & Eagles, G. 2014. Constraining South Atlantic growth with seafloor spreading data. *Tectonics* 33(9): 1848-1873. doi:10.1002/2014tc003644
   - Moulin, M., Aslanian, D. & Unternehr, P. 2010. A new starting point for the South and Equatorial Atlantic Ocean. *Earth-Science Reviews* 98(1-2): 1-37. doi:10.1016/j.earscirev.2009.08.001 (citation checked; abstract not read)
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The ocean opened from south to north over about 35 million years; the date depends on where along the coast you stand."

## 8. Bay of Biscay, with the Iberia-Newfoundland rift

1. **Other names:** southern North Atlantic rift; Bay of Biscay-Pyrenean rift system; Iberian margin.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening that then stopped (Bay of Biscay). West of Iberia the rift became part of the North Atlantic, which is still widening.
3. **Ages.**
   - *Rifting:* the Bay of Biscay and Pyrenees are "a Lower Cretaceous rift system" with both oceanic and extremely stretched continental parts (Tugend et al. 2014) [abstract]; Early Cretaceous is 143 to 100.5 Ma on the ICS chart [memory]. Earlier, weaker Triassic and Jurassic stretching affected the whole region; the rift and early spreading history from 200 to 83 Ma "remains highly disputed" (Nirrengarten et al. 2018) [abstract].
   - *Sea floor in the Bay of Biscay:* from magnetic stripe M0 to stripe A33o, 118 to 80 Ma (Sibuet et al. 2004) [abstract]. Then it stopped.
   - *Disagreement:* the "J" magnetic anomaly off Iberia and Newfoundland, long used as the M0 time line, is probably not a time line at all (Nirrengarten et al. 2018) [abstract]. This undermines the 118 Ma start. Mantle rock was pulled up to the sea bed and spreading spread northward in a V shape (same source) [abstract]. A first-sea-floor date of about 112 Ma west of Iberia is often quoted [memory].
   - **App: 145 to 80** (Early Cretaceous rifting, rounded, to the end of spreading in the Bay). Medium confidence at both ends.
4. **What pulled apart:** Iberia (Spain and Portugal) swung away from France, opening the Bay of Biscay, and separated from Newfoundland on its west side.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: northern Spain, Cantabrian coast and Basque-Cantabrian basin `[42.3, -9.5, 43.9, -1.5]`; Aquitaine and Parentis basins, south-west France `[43.0, -2.0, 45.5, 0.5]`; Portugal, Lusitanian basin `[37.0, -9.8, 41.0, -8.0]`; Pyrenees, where the rift basins were later crushed into the mountain belt (Tugend et al. 2014) [abstract] `[42.0, -2.0, 43.5, 3.3]`. The Armorican (Brittany) coast faces the Bay but I know of no rift rocks onshore there; leave it out. Newfoundland's side is offshore (Grand Banks). Inland limit: about 100 km. (b) Provinces: **502 Iberian Rifted Margin** (35.9 to 51.5 N, 14.7 to 1.9 W), **454 Cantabrian-Pyrenean Belt** (orogenic belt; holds the squeezed rift basins), **639 West Atlantic Rifted Margin** and **675 Orphan Knoll** (Newfoundland side, offshore).
6. **At the surface today:** the square-cut shape of the Bay; Cretaceous limestones and sandstones of the Basque coast; rare outcrops of mantle rock (lherzolite, named after Étang de Lers in the Pyrenees) brought up when the crust was stretched to nothing [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "In the Early Cretaceous the crust between Spain and France was stretched until it broke, and Spain swung away to open the Bay of Biscay. The Bay stopped widening about 80 million years ago, and the same movement later reversed to raise the Pyrenees."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Tugend, J., Manatschal, G., Kusznir, N.J., Masini, E., Mohn, G. & Thinon, I. 2014. Formation and deformation of hyperextended rift systems: insights from rift domain mapping in the Bay of Biscay-Pyrenees. *Tectonics* 33(7): 1239-1276. doi:10.1002/2014tc003529
   - Sibuet, J.-C., Srivastava, S.P. & Spakman, W. 2004. Pyrenean orogeny and plate kinematics. *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* 109(B8): article 2003JB002514 (as Crossref gives it). doi:10.1029/2003jb002514
   - Nirrengarten, M., Manatschal, G., Tugend, J., Kusznir, N. & Sauter, D. 2018. Kinematic evolution of the southern North Atlantic: implications for the formation of hyperextended rift systems. *Tectonics* 37(1): 89-118. doi:10.1002/2017tc004495
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "How and exactly when Spain moved is still argued over; the dates could shift by ten million years." The last clause of the visitor text ("later reversed to raise the Pyrenees") rests on Sibuet et al. and Tugend et al. [abstract].

## 9. North Sea rift

1. **Other names:** Viking Graben, Central Graben and Moray Firth rift (its three arms); North Sea rift system.
2. **Kind:** rift that failed.
3. **Ages.** Two rift phases in the northern North Sea: late Permian to Early Triassic, and Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous; toward the end of the second, activity moved north as the stretching that would open the North Atlantic took over (Phillips et al. 2019) [abstract]. On the ICS chart the late Permian begins at about 260 Ma and the Late Jurassic runs from about 161.5 to 143 Ma [memory]. The Late Jurassic phase is the main one, and its sediments are the source of North Sea oil [memory]. **App: 260 to 140** if both phases are meant, with a note that there was a long quiet interval between; **or 161 to 140** for the main phase alone. I recommend the second for a visitor tile. Rests on the ages of sediments that thicken into the faults, from boreholes and seismic surveys.
4. **What pulled apart:** the crust between Britain and Norway stretched and sagged along three arms meeting in the middle of the North Sea, but did not break. The break came farther west and north instead, as the Atlantic.
5. **Extent on land. Weak.** The rift is almost entirely under the sea. (a) Boxes [memory], for coasts beside the rift arms: Moray Firth coast, north-east Scotland, where Late Jurassic boulder beds shed from an active rift fault are exposed near Helmsdale `[57.4, -4.5, 58.7, -1.7]`; Netherlands and north-west Germany, over buried Jurassic-Cretaceous rift basins that were later squeezed `[51.5, 3.5, 53.8, 9.0]`. Western Norway and Shetland face the Viking Graben but have essentially no rift rocks on land; I would not include them. (b) **The map has no North Sea rift province** under any rift or margin type (searched by name). Province 508 "North European Rifted Margin" is the Atlantic margin, a different event (entry 10).
6. **At the surface today:** very little. The Helmsdale boulder beds on the Sutherland coast; the Jurassic coast of Yorkshire belongs to a neighbouring basin of the same age [memory]. The rift is known to the public mainly as the home of North Sea oil and gas.
7. **For visitors:** "In the Late Jurassic, about 160 to 140 million years ago, the crust under what is now the North Sea was stretched along three great troughs. The stretching stopped before the land broke apart, and the troughs, now buried under the sea bed, hold the North Sea's oil."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Phillips, T.B., Fazlikhani, H., Gawthorpe, R.L., Fossen, H., Jackson, C.A.-L., Bell, R.E., Faleide, J.I. & Rotevatn, A. 2019. The influence of structural inheritance and multiphase extension on rift development, the northern North Sea. *Tectonics* 38(12): 4099-4126. doi:10.1029/2019tc005756
   - Ziegler, P.A. 1992. North Sea rift system. *Tectonophysics* 208(1-3): 55-75. doi:10.1016/0040-1951(92)90336-5 (citation checked; abstract not read)
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "This rift lies offshore; on land there is almost nothing to see of it." An honest tile for a place on land may not be possible; consider showing it only for coastal points in the two boxes, or not at all.

## 10. Labrador Sea and North-East Atlantic (Greenland leaves North America, then Europe)

1. **Other names:** opening of the Norwegian-Greenland Sea; North Atlantic Igneous Province (the lava); British and Irish Paleogene (or "Tertiary") volcanic province; opening of Baffin Bay.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening. The Labrador Sea arm later stopped; the North-East Atlantic is still widening (through Iceland).
3. **Ages, by segment.**
   - *Labrador Sea, first sea floor:* **disputed.** Roest & Srivastava 1989 put initial opening at 92 Ma [abstract]. Chalmers & Laursen 1995 argued that true sea floor began only in the Paleocene, around magnetic stripe 27, about 62 Ma [memory; citation checked, abstract not read]. Oakey & Chalmers 2012 model Greenland's motion in a Paleocene stage (stripes C27N to C25N) and an Eocene stage (C24N to C13N) and find a Paleocene spreading centre in Baffin Bay [abstract]. C13N is about 33 Ma [memory], when the Labrador Sea is generally said to have stopped widening [memory].
   - *North-East Atlantic, rifting:* stretching in the Late Cretaceous to Paleocene and earlier; volcanic rifting began in the Paleocene (Gernigon et al. 2020) [abstract]. The lavas are usually dated to about 62 to 54 Ma [memory; Storey et al. 2007, citation checked, abstract not read].
   - *North-East Atlantic, first sea floor:* 54 Ma (Gaina et al. 2009) [abstract]; Early Eocene, at different times in different places, starting as isolated segments (Gernigon et al. 2020) [abstract].
   - **App: 62 to 54** (start of the lavas to the first sea floor between Greenland and Europe). For the Labrador coast and south-west Greenland a separate pair would be more honest: 92 or 62 (disputed) to 33.
4. **What pulled apart:** Greenland first from Labrador and Baffin Island, then from north-west Europe (Norway, the Faroes, Britain and Ireland). Europe and North America, which had still been joined through Greenland, were finally separated.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: Inner Hebrides and west Scotland (Skye, Rum, Mull, Ardnamurchan, Arran) `[55.2, -7.0, 57.8, -5.0]`; Antrim, Northern Ireland, with the Mourne and Carlingford centres `[54.0, -7.0, 55.4, -5.4]`; Faroe Islands `[61.3, -7.8, 62.5, -6.2]`; East Greenland lava coast `[66.0, -42.0, 76.0, -19.0]`; West Greenland, Disko and Nuussuaq `[68.5, -56.0, 72.5, -50.0]`; Labrador coast `[52.0, -62.0, 60.5, -55.0]`; south-west Greenland coast `[59.5, -52.0, 67.0, -43.0]`. Norway's coast faces the margin, but its rift basins are offshore; leave it out or flag it as offshore-only. Iceland is not rifted continent but new crust made on the ridge; it needs its own treatment. Inland limit: the lava fields and intrusive centres themselves, generally within 100 km of the coast. (b) Provinces: **508 North European Rifted Margin** (50.1 to 77.1 N; mostly offshore), **911 North Greenland Rifted Margin**, **660 South Greenland Rifted Margin**, **640 Baffin-Labrador Rifted Margin**, **910 Jan Mayen** (a sliver of continent left in mid-ocean), and for Iceland **893, 894, 906, 907**. ## 11. Australia leaves Antarctica (Southern Ocean)

1. **Other names:** Australian Southern Rift System; opening of the Australian-Antarctic Basin; Otway, Bass and Gippsland rift basins.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening, very slow at first.
3. **Ages.**
   - *Rifting began:* not dated in any abstract I read. Usually given as Middle to Late Jurassic, about 165 to 160 Ma, from the oldest sediments in the Bight and Otway rift basins **[memory]**.
   - *First sea floor:* usually given as about 83 Ma **[memory]**. But the magnetic stripes older than chron 31 (68.7 Ma) in this basin may not be true time lines at all; spreading was "extremely slow" from chron 31 (68.7 Ma) to chron 24 (53.3 Ma) (Tikku & Cande 1999) [abstract].
   - *Rapid spreading established:* about 43 Ma (Whittaker et al. 2013) [abstract].
   - The early history "is not well established" (Williams et al. 2011) [abstract].
   - **App: 160 to 43**, start = rift sediments [memory], end = the change to rapid spreading [abstract]. A long, vague span, but the honest one. If one date is wanted for a tile: "sea floor certainly forming by about 69 million years ago".
4. **What pulled apart:** the south coast of Australia from Antarctica (Wilkes Land), the last two large pieces of Gondwana.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: south coast of Western Australia and the Great Australian Bight `[-35.5, 115.0, -31.0, 135.5]`; Otway coast and ranges, south-east South Australia and western Victoria `[-39.2, 139.5, -37.2, 144.8]`; Gippsland and the Strzelecki Ranges `[-39.2, 145.0, -37.4, 148.8]`; Tasmania `[-43.8, 144.5, -40.5, 148.6]`. Inland limit: about 100 km, except where the Otway and Gippsland rift fills are uplifted on land. (b) Provinces: **425 South Australia Rifted Margin** (49.2 to 33.1 S, 114.5 to 147.4 E), **371 Bass Straight** (as spelled in the table; narrow rift), **118 East Antarctica Rifted Margin**.
6. **At the surface today:** the Otway Ranges and the Great Ocean Road cliffs are cut in Early Cretaceous sandstone that filled the rift valley, with dinosaur fossils at Dinosaur Cove and Inverloch; the long straight cliffs of the Great Australian Bight (the limestone is younger, laid on the new margin) [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "Australia and Antarctica were the last large pieces of the southern supercontinent to part. A rift valley ran along this coast in the age of dinosaurs; the sea floor between the two continents grew very slowly at first and has widened quickly only since about 43 million years ago."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Whittaker, J.M., Williams, S.E. & Müller, R.D. 2013. Revised tectonic evolution of the Eastern Indian Ocean. *Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems* 14(6): 1891-1909. doi:10.1002/ggge.20120

9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The separation was slow and its early dates are uncertain." The final opening of a deep seaway south of Tasmania is told in the project's Antarctic glaciation turning point; do not repeat it here.

## 12. Tasman Sea

1. **Other names:** separation of Zealandia (the mostly drowned continent that carries New Zealand and New Caledonia) from Australia.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening that then stopped.
3. **Ages.** Thirteen continental blocks moved as small plates between 90 and 64 Ma; rifting ran from south to north in stages, and several rifts failed; sea-floor spreading is modelled from 73.6 Ma to 52 Ma, "when spreading ceased" (Gaina et al. 1998) [abstract]. The oldest sea floor is usually given as about 83 or 84 Ma [memory]. Zealandia's crust was thinned widely in the Late Cretaceous before breakup, which is why 94% of it is now under water (Mortimer et al. 2017) [abstract]. **App: 90 to 52** (start of block movement to end of spreading). Rests on magnetic stripes and fracture zones.
4. **What pulled apart:** Zealandia from the east coast of Australia.

## 13. European Cenozoic rift system (Rhine Graben)

1. **Other names:** ECRIS; Upper Rhine Graben, Lower Rhine (Roer Valley) Graben, Hessian grabens, Limagne, Bresse and Rhône grabens, Eger (Ohře) Graben.
2. **Kind:** rift that has not broken the continent and is only weakly active now. Of the four kinds, "rift still active" fits the Upper and Lower Rhine parts best (they have earthquakes [memory]); the French grabens are closer to "rift that failed".
3. **Ages (Upper Rhine Graben).** Separate basins formed in the middle and late Eocene; they joined into one graben during Oligocene stretching; sideways (strike-slip) movement in the late Oligocene; main sinking of the northern part in the early Miocene; uplift and erosion of the southern part in the middle Miocene; sideways movement again in the Pliocene and Quaternary (Schumacher 2002) [abstract]. The middle Eocene begins at about 48 Ma on the ICS chart [memory]. **App: 45 to 0.** Rests on the ages of the sediments filling the graben. Dèzes et al. 2004 is the standard review of the whole system and ties it to the Alps and Pyrenees [title]; Michon & Merle 2005 published a formal disagreement with it [Crossref listing], so the cause is disputed; I did not read either abstract.
4. **What pulled apart:** the crust of western Europe north and west of the Alps stretched east-west by a few kilometres along a chain of troughs from the Mediterranean to the North Sea [memory for "a few kilometres"].
5. **Extent on land.** Entirely on land, and well defined. (a) Boxes [memory]: Upper Rhine Graben with the Vosges and Black Forest shoulders, Basel to Frankfurt `[47.4, 7.0, 50.2, 9.0]`; Lower Rhine Embayment and Roer Valley Graben, Bonn to the southern Netherlands `[50.5, 5.3, 52.0, 7.3]`; Hessian grabens `[50.0, 8.5, 51.7, 10.0]`; Limagne and the Massif Central grabens `[45.0, 2.8, 46.7, 4.4]`; Bresse, Saône and Rhône grabens `[44.0, 4.4, 47.6, 5.9]`; Eger Graben, north-west Czech Republic `[50.0, 12.2, 50.9, 14.6]`. (b) **The map has no province for any part of this system** (searched "Rhine", "Rhenish", "Massif Central", "Bohemian", and the rift types). The boxes are all we have.
6. **At the surface today:** the flat-floored Rhine valley, 30 to 40 km wide, between the facing scarps of the Vosges and the Black Forest; the Kaiserstuhl volcano; hot springs at Baden-Baden; the Limagne plain below the Chaîne des Puys; the brown-coal pits of the Lower Rhine [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "From about 45 million years ago the crust of western Europe was stretched along a chain of troughs running from the Rhône valley to the Netherlands. Here a strip of land dropped between two faults to make a rift valley, and it is still moving slightly today."

## 14. South China Sea

1. **Other names:** Nan Hai; opening of the East and Southwest Sub-basins.
2. **Kind:** ocean opening that then stopped. (The Hasterok map types its floor "oceanic back-arc basin"; the cause is debated, see below.)
3. **Ages.**
   - *Rifting began:* not dated in an abstract I read; usually given as latest Cretaceous to Eocene [memory].
   - *First sea floor:* about 33 Ma in the north-east, varying by 1 to 2 million years along the northern edge; a ridge jump at about 23.6 Ma, when spreading also began in the Southwest Sub-basin and grew south-westward until about 21.5 Ma (Li et al. 2014, from deep-towed magnetic surveys and drill cores) [abstract]. Briais et al. 1993 had 32 Ma [abstract].
   - *Stopped:* about 15 Ma in the east and about 16 Ma in the south-west (Li et al. 2014) [abstract]; about 15.5 Ma (Briais et al. 1993) [abstract].
   - **App: 33 to 15** (sea-floor spreading, start to finish). Good agreement between the two dating papers.
   - *Cause, disputed:* either blocks of south-east Asia were squeezed out sideways by India's collision, or the crust was pulled by a subducting plate to the south. Jolivet et al. 1994 note that the first idea "is not accepted by several workers" [abstract].
4. **What pulled apart:** a strip of the South China margin (now under the Spratly Islands, Palawan and north-west Borneo) from the coast of South China and Vietnam [memory].
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: South China coast, Guangxi, Guangdong, Hong Kong and Hainan `[18.0, 107.0, 24.0, 118.0]`; Vietnam's coast `[8.5, 105.5, 17.5, 109.7]`; Palawan and the north-west Borneo coast, the piece that moved away `[5.0, 113.0, 12.0, 120.0]`. Inland limit: 100 to 150 km; rift basins of this age on land are small. (b) Provinces: **278 Pearl River Mouth Basin** (passive margin; 16.7 to 25.8 N, 106.5 to 121.4 E; a good match for the Chinese side), **280 South China Sea Rift** (the sea floor itself). Nothing found for the Vietnamese or Palawan sides.
6. **At the surface today:** little on land beyond the outline of the coast and its many drowned islands; young basalt fields on Hainan and the Leizhou Peninsula post-date the opening [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "The South China Sea opened when a strip of land broke away from this coast and moved south. New sea floor formed from about 33 to about 15 million years ago; then the sea stopped widening."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Li, C.-F., Xu, X., Lin, J., et al. [41 authors] 2014. Ages and magnetic structures of the South China Sea constrained by deep tow magnetic surveys and IODP Expedition 349. *Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems* 15(12): 4958-4983. doi:10.1002/2014gc005567

9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The date is for the sea floor. The land along the coast had begun to stretch millions of years earlier."

## 15. Sea of Japan

1. **Other names:** East Sea; Japan Sea back-arc basin; opening of the Japan and Yamato basins.
2. **Kind:** back-arc sea opening.
3. **Ages.** "The Japan Sea opened in the early Miocene" as a pull-apart between two great sideways-moving fault zones; most of the sea is stretched continental crust, with true sea floor only in the Japan Basin (Jolivet et al. 1994) [abstract]. The early Miocene is 23.0 to 16.0 Ma on the ICS chart [memory]. Magnetic directions in rocks show that south-west Japan swung clockwise and north-east Japan anticlockwise as the sea opened (Otofuji et al. 1985) [title for the method; the sense and the often-quoted date of about 15 Ma are memory]. Stretching is commonly said to have begun in the late Oligocene, about 30 to 25 Ma, and to have ended by about 15 Ma [memory]. **App: 23 to 15.** Disagreement: fast, late opening (around 15 Ma, from rock magnetism) against slower opening from about 25 Ma (from drilling) [memory]; the title of Van Horne et al. 2017 ("some unsolved issues") signals that this is open.
4. **What pulled apart:** the Japanese islands were a strip of the Asian mainland's edge; they moved away from what is now Korea and the Russian Far East, and the sea opened behind them.
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: the Sea of Japan side of Honshu, including the "Green Tuff" belt of north-east Japan `[34.0, 130.5, 41.6, 141.0]`; western Hokkaido `[41.3, 139.3, 45.6, 142.2]`; east coast of Korea, Pohang and Ulsan basins `[35.0, 128.6, 38.7, 129.7]`; Primorye coast, Russia `[42.3, 130.5, 47.5, 139.5]`. (b) Provinces: **345 Sea of Japan** (continental back-arc basin; 33.5 to 53.3 N, 127.6 to 143.2 E; this looks like the right one and seems to include land margins), **288 Japan Sea** (the oceanic part). 6. **At the surface today:** the green-tinted altered volcanic rocks of the Green Tuff belt along the Sea of Japan side; the Oga Peninsula and Sado Island; the Fossa Magna, the great break across central Honshu between the two halves that swung apart [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "Japan was once part of the edge of mainland Asia. In the early Miocene, about 23 to 15 million years ago, the islands were pulled away and the Sea of Japan opened behind them."

## 16. Western Mediterranean basins

1. **Other names:** Liguro-Provençal (Ligurian-Provençal) Basin, Gulf of Lion margin, Valencia Trough, Algerian Basin, Alboran Sea, Tyrrhenian Sea; rotation of Corsica and Sardinia.
2. **Kind:** back-arc sea opening (several small seas, one after another).
3. **Ages.**
   - *Began:* trench retreat and back-arc stretching from 30 Ma on (Faccenna et al. 2004) [abstract]; several back-arc basins "all started to form in the Oligocene" (Romagny et al. 2020, a reconstruction from 35 Ma to the present) [abstract].
   - *Liguro-Provençal sea floor and the swing of Corsica and Sardinia away from France:* early Miocene, commonly about 21 to 16 Ma **[memory]**; the dating papers are Gattacceca et al. 2007 and Speranza et al. 2002 [titles: "Miocene rotation of Sardinia"; "Age of the Corsica-Sardinia rotation and Liguro-Provençal Basin spreading"], abstracts not read.

## 17. Red Sea and Gulf of Aden

1. **Other names:** Afro-Arabian rift; separation of Arabia from Africa; Gulf of Suez rift (the northern, failed end); Sheba Ridge (the Gulf of Aden's spreading ridge).

## 18. East African Rift

3. **Ages, by segment.**
   - Volcanism since the late Eocene, at first in pulses over areas 500 to 1,000 km wide; then scattered but simultaneous activity along all four branches since 25 to 27 Ma; then, at about 12 to 12.5 Ma, volcanism resumed everywhere at once, "followed by a general extensional deformation" (Michon et al. 2022) [abstract]. The older view was about 12 Ma or younger [memory].

## 19. Basin and Range

1. **Other names:** Basin and Range Province; Great Basin extension; in Mexico, the Mexican Basin and Range.
2. **Kind:** rift still active (a "wide rift": stretching spread over hundreds of kilometres instead of one valley).
3. **Ages.** The province is a composite of several stretching episodes with different causes: Late Paleocene to Middle Miocene stretching above thickened crust, which uncovered the "metamorphic core complexes"; and late Early Miocene and younger stretching in the north-western part, including the Great Basin (Dickinson 2002) [abstract]. In north-central Nevada there was little or no surface deformation until about 16 to 17 Ma, when major stretching began (Colgan & Henry 2009) [abstract]. Reconstructions of the whole region are run from 36 Ma (Bahadori et al. 2018) [abstract]. **App: 17 to 0** for the Great Basin (the block-faulting that made today's ranges). The earlier episodes (from about 55 Ma on Dickinson's reading; from 36 Ma in the reconstructions) should be named in the caveat. For the southern Basin and Range (Arizona, Sonora) the main stretching is older, late Oligocene to middle Miocene [memory]; if a separate pair is wanted there, 28 to 10 [memory]. The frequent earthquakes and the widening measured by GPS show it is still active [memory].
4. **What pulled apart:** the crust of western North America between the Sierra Nevada and the Colorado Plateau has been stretched to roughly twice its former width in places [memory for the amount], breaking into tilted blocks.
5. **Extent on land.** Entirely on land. (a) Boxes [memory]: Great Basin, Nevada, western Utah, south-east Oregon and eastern California `[35.5, -120.5, 43.5, -111.0]`; southern Arizona, south-west New Mexico and the lower Colorado River `[31.0, -115.5, 35.5, -108.0]`; Mexican Basin and Range, Sonora and Chihuahua to Durango `[23.0, -111.5, 31.5, -103.5]`. (b) Provinces: **650 Northern Basin and Range**, **665 Southern Basin and Range** (both "wide rift", group Basin and Range). Possibly also **658 Columbia Basin and Range**, **572 Northern Rocky Mountains**, **606 Mesa Central** and **578 Sierra Madre Oriental** (all typed "wide rift"; 578 is grouped with the Basin and Range in the table despite its name). I would start with 650 and 665 only.
6. **At the surface today:** hundreds of parallel mountain ranges separated by flat desert valleys; Death Valley, below sea level; the Wasatch Front above Salt Lake City and the east face of the Sierra Nevada, both active fault scarps; Great Salt Lake; hot springs [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "The land between the Sierra Nevada and the Rocky Mountains is being stretched from east to west. Over about the last 17 million years it has broken into long blocks that tilted to form today's parallel ranges and valleys, and it is still stretching."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Dickinson, W.R. 2002. The Basin and Range Province as a composite extensional domain. *International Geology Review* 44(1): 1-38. doi:10.2747/0020-6814.44.1.1
   - Colgan, J.P. & Henry, C.D. 2009. Rapid middle Miocene collapse of the Mesozoic orogenic plateau in north-central Nevada. *International Geology Review* 51(9-11): 920-961. doi:10.1080/00206810903056731
   - Bahadori, A., Holt, W.E. & Rasbury, E.T. 2018. Reconstruction modeling of crustal thickness and paleotopography of western North America since 36 Ma. *Geosphere* 14(3): 1207-1231. doi:10.1130/ges01604.1
   - **UNVERIFIED:** McQuarrie, N. & Wernicke, B.P. 2005. An animated tectonic reconstruction of southwestern North America since 36 Ma. *Geosphere* 1: 147-172. (My Crossref search returned other papers; details are from memory.)
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The stretching came in several episodes over about 50 million years and at different times in different places; the date given is for the episode that made the present ranges of Nevada and Utah."

## 20. Rio Grande Rift

1. **Other names:** none in common use; its basins include the San Luis, Española, Albuquerque and Mesilla basins.
2. **Kind:** rift still active, slowly. (Dickinson 2002 treats its early phases as part of the Basin and Range composite [abstract].)
3. **Ages.** The rift's shoulders cooled rapidly, as faulting lifted and stripped them, from about 28 Ma to Recent in the Sawatch Range and Sangre de Cristo Mountains (Colorado), about 21 to 5 Ma around the Albuquerque basin, and about 17 to 8 Ma in southern New Mexico; taken together, stretching was roughly simultaneous along more than 850 km from about 20 to 10 Ma (Ricketts et al. 2016) [abstract]. The paper's title gives "25-10 Ma". **App: 28 to 0**, or **25 to 10** for the main phase as the title has it. Rests on apatite cooling ages from the flanks, not on dated basin sediments. Whether the rift opened all at once or unzipped northward is the disagreement; Ricketts et al. argue against unzipping [abstract].
4. **What pulled apart:** the Colorado Plateau on the west moved slightly away from the Great Plains on the east, dropping a chain of basins between them.
5. **Extent on land.** Entirely on land. (a) Boxes [memory]: central Colorado, upper Arkansas valley and San Luis Valley `[37.0, -107.0, 39.6, -105.0]`; New Mexico, Taos to Las Cruces `[31.7, -107.8, 37.0, -105.2]`; El Paso, Ciudad Juárez and northern Chihuahua `[29.5, -107.3, 31.9, -104.5]`. (b) Province **574 Rio Grande Rift** (narrow rift; 31.2 to 38.2 N, 107.5 to 105.4 W). A good match; use the province.
6. **At the surface today:** the Rio Grande flows down the rift from Colorado to El Paso; the Sandia Mountains above Albuquerque and the Sangre de Cristo range are its uplifted shoulders; the Rio Grande Gorge near Taos; young volcanoes and lava flows on the valley floor [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "The valley of the Rio Grande is a rift, a strip of crust that dropped as the land on either side pulled apart. Most of the movement happened between about 25 and 10 million years ago, and it continues slowly."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Ricketts, J.W., Kelley, S.A., Karlstrom, K.E., Schmandt, B., Donahue, M.S. & van Wijk, J. 2016. Synchronous opening of the Rio Grande rift along its entire length at 25-10 Ma supported by apatite (U-Th)/He and fission-track thermochronology, and evaluation of possible driving mechanisms. *Geological Society of America Bulletin* 128(3-4): 397-424. doi:10.1130/b31223.1 (Crossref gives the online year 2015.)
   - Dickinson 2002, as in entry 19.
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "'Continues slowly' is from memory in this dossier; the source read supports cooling 'to Recent' in Colorado only." Only one dating source was read; a second (basin-fill ages) should be added before the entry is treated as vetted.

## 21. Gulf of California

1. **Other names:** Sea of Cortez (Mar de Cortés); Salton Trough (its dry northern end).
2. **Kind:** ocean opening, still widening.
3. **Ages.** Subduction west of southern Baja California ended at 12.5 Ma, and stretching of the continent has taken up part of the plate motion since; but the plate boundary settled into the Gulf only at about 6 Ma, and was fully there by 4.7 Ma (Oskin et al. 2001) [abstract]. Sea water: at 8 Ma only an embayment at the Gulf's southern mouth; by about 7 Ma farther north; the northern 500 km and more flooded by 6.5 to 6.3 Ma (Umhoefer et al. 2018) [abstract]. True sea floor in the southern Gulf dates from about 3.5 Ma [memory; Lizarralde et al. 2007, citation checked, abstract not read]. **App: 12.5 to 0.** Rests on dated volcanic ash layers matched across the Gulf and on the first marine sediments. No serious disagreement on the 6 Ma step; an older "proto-Gulf" of 12 to 6 Ma is argued over in detail.
4. **What pulled apart:** the Baja California peninsula from mainland Mexico. Baja now rides on the Pacific plate and is moving north-west, obliquely, away from the mainland (Oskin et al. 2001; Bennett & Oskin 2014) [abstract].
5. **Extent on land.** (a) Boxes [memory]: Baja California peninsula `[22.5, -116.5, 32.7, -109.0]`; coast of Sonora, Sinaloa and Nayarit `[21.0, -113.5, 32.0, -105.0]`; Salton Trough and the Colorado delta, California and Baja California `[31.5, -116.5, 34.0, -114.3]`. Inland limit on the mainland side: about 100 km. (b) Province **677 Gulf of California Coast** (wide rift; 20.5 to 34.0 N, 116.6 to 105.0 W). A good match. **592 Baja Borderland** is the Pacific side and an older story; do not use.
6. **At the surface today:** the long narrow Gulf and its steep eastern escarpment on Baja; the Salton Sea, below sea level; geothermal fields and mud volcanoes in the Imperial and Mexicali valleys; the San Andreas Fault, which carries the same plate motion on northward [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "Baja California was once joined to mainland Mexico. It began to pull away about 12 million years ago, the sea flooded in between about 8 and 6 million years ago, and the Gulf is still widening as Baja slides north-west."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Oskin, M., Stock, J. & Martín-Barajas, A. 2001. Rapid localization of Pacific-North America plate motion in the Gulf of California. *Geology* 29(5): 459-462 (Crossref gives first page only). doi:10.1130/0091-7613(2001)029<0459:rlopna>2.0.co;2
   - Umhoefer, P.J., Darin, M.H., Bennett, S.E.K., Skinner, L.A., Dorsey, R.J. & Oskin, M.E. 2018. Breaching of strike-slip faults and successive flooding of pull-apart basins to form the Gulf of California seaway from ca. 8-6 Ma. *Geology* 46(8): 695-698. doi:10.1130/g40242.1
   - Bennett, S.E.K. & Oskin, M.E. 2014. Oblique rifting ruptures continents: example from the Gulf of California shear zone. *Geology* 42(3): 215-218. doi:10.1130/g34904.1
   - Lizarralde, D., Axen, G.J., Brown, H.E., et al. [11 authors] 2007. Variation in styles of rifting in the Gulf of California. *Nature* 448(7152): 466-469. doi:10.1038/nature06035 (citation checked; abstract not read)
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "The two sides are sliding past each other as much as pulling apart."

## 22. Baikal Rift

1. **Other names:** Baikal Rift Zone (or System); includes the Tunka, Barguzin, Muya and Chara basins and, in Mongolia, Lake Khövsgöl.
2. **Kind:** rift still active.
3. **Ages. Disputed at the start.** A history of about 30 million years: an Oligocene "early stage", perhaps dominated by sideways movement, made early basins unlike today's; most of the true rift basins seem to have begun later, in the Late Miocene or Pliocene (Petit & Déverchère 2006) [abstract]. Against this, Mats & Perepelova 2011 place the onset in the Late Cretaceous, with three stages: Late Cretaceous to Oligocene, late Oligocene to early Pliocene, and later [abstract]. The Late Cretaceous began at 100.5 Ma and ended at 66 Ma [memory]. **App: 30 to 0** (the majority view, as I read it). Rests on the ages of sediments on the lake's shores and beneath its floor.
4. **What pulled apart:** the crust along the edge of the ancient Siberian craton is being pulled away from the land to the south-east (Petit & Déverchère: the rift follows the old join bounding the craton) [abstract].
5. **Extent on land.** Entirely on land. (a) Boxes [memory]: Lake Baikal and its shores `[51.0, 102.5, 56.5, 111.0]`; north-eastern basins, Barguzin to Chara `[54.0, 109.0, 57.8, 120.0]`; Tunka valley and Lake Khövsgöl `[50.0, 99.5, 52.6, 103.5]`. (b) Province **135 Baikal Rift** (narrow rift; 51.4 to 56.0 N, 103.3 to 110.0 E). A good match for the lake itself; it does not seem to reach the north-eastern basins or Khövsgöl.
6. **At the surface today:** Lake Baikal, the world's deepest lake and, by the age of the sediments beneath it, among the oldest; steep fault-bounded shores; hot springs; frequent earthquakes [memory].
7. **For visitors:** "Lake Baikal fills a rift, a place where the crust of Asia is being pulled apart. The rift began to form about 30 million years ago, deepened greatly in the last few million years, and is still widening."
8. **Key sources.**
   - Petit, C. & Déverchère, J. 2006. Structure and evolution of the Baikal rift: a synthesis. *Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems* 7(11): article 2006GC001265. doi:10.1029/2006gc001265
   - Mats, V.D. & Perepelova, T.I. 2011. A new perspective on evolution of the Baikal Rift. *Geoscience Frontiers* 2(3): 349-365. doi:10.1016/j.gsf.2011.06.002 (open access)
9. **Caveats for visitors:** "One group of researchers argues that the rift began far earlier, more than 66 million years ago." "World's deepest lake" and "still widening" are [memory].

## Considered and left out

### Opening of the Rheic Ocean: leave out
The standard review is Nance, R.D., Gutiérrez-Alonso, G., Keppie, J.D., Linnemann, U., Murphy, J.B., Quesada, C., Strachan, R.A. & Woodcock, N.H. 2010. Evolution of the Rheic Ocean. *Gondwana Research* 17(2-3): 194-222. doi:10.1016/j.gr.2009.08.001 (citation checked; abstract not read). From memory: the ocean opened in the latest Cambrian to Early Ordovician, about 490 to 480 Ma, when slivers of land including Avalonia (now southern Britain, parts of Newfoundland, Nova Scotia and New England) left the northern edge of Gondwana **[memory]**. **No honest region exists.** Both margins were destroyed or heavily overprinted when the ocean closed to build the Variscan, Alleghanian and Ouachita mountains; what survives is scattered rift-related volcanic rock inside those belts in Iberia, Bohemia, Mexico and elsewhere, which cannot be drawn as a margin. Those places are better served by the mountain-building entries in the companion dossier. I read no source in this work that would support a date or a region, so I will not write a visitor text.

### Opening of the Neo-Tethys (Cimmerian blocks leave Gondwana): optional, not recommended
The Early Permian (290 Ma) Panjal Traps of Kashmir erupted in a continental rift that went on to become "a nascent ocean basin that led to seafloor spreading and the formation of the Neotethys Ocean and the ribbon-like continent Cimmeria" (Shellnutt 2017) [abstract]. Citation checked: Shellnutt, J.G. 2017. The Panjal Traps. *Geological Society, London, Special Publications* 463(1): 59-86. doi:10.1144/sp463.4 (Crossref gives the online year 2017). Also checked, abstract not read: Stampfli, G.M. & Borel, G.D. 2002. A plate tectonic model for the Paleozoic and Mesozoic constrained by dynamic plate boundaries and restored synthetic oceanic isochrons. *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 196(1-2): 17-33. doi:10.1016/s0012-821x(01)00588-x. An honest region exists only in small pieces: the Panjal Traps themselves (Kashmir valley and Pir Panjal, roughly `[32.5, 73.5, 35.0, 76.5]` [memory]) and, from memory, Permian rift rocks in Oman and the Permian basins of north-west Australia. The ocean is gone and the margin is now folded into the Himalaya. If wanted, the Panjal Traps would sit better in the existing lava list (name, 290 Ma, the Kashmir box, with a summary saying the lavas mark the rift that opened a vanished ocean) than as an ocean-opening entry. Hasterok provinces: none suitable found; **147 Sibumasu Terrane** is typed "passive margin" and is one of the Cimmerian blocks, but it is the piece that left, now in south-east Asia, and tying a visitor in Thailand to a rift in Gondwana needs more explanation than a tile can carry.

### Drake Passage and Tasmanian Gateway: no entry
These are seaways, and their importance is for ocean circulation and climate, which the project already tells in the Antarctic glaciation turning point. As rifting events they have almost no land: the Drake Passage lies between Tierra del Fuego and the Antarctic Peninsula, with the small Scotia Sea plates between; the Tasmanian Gateway is the last stage of entry 11, and Tasmania is already in that entry's region. A separate entry would repeat the turning point for very few visitors. I checked no source on their dates in this work.

### Other rifts in the Hasterok map that could be added later
Not researched here; listed so that nobody assumes they were rejected: **99 West Antarctic Rift** and **115 Ross Rift** (wide rifts); **362 West Siberian Rift** (failed rift, tied in the literature to the Siberian Traps [memory]); **459 Dneipr-Donets Basin** (failed rift, Devonian [memory]); **747 Amazonian Graben** (failed rift); **538 Mahanadi** and **550 Godavari** rifts (failed, Permian-Triassic [memory]); **94, 122 Lambert Rift** (Antarctica); **453 Panonian Basin** (wide rift behind the Carpathians, Miocene [memory]; a strong candidate for a back-arc entry in central Europe, where many people live); **310 Woodlark Rift** (active, Papua New Guinea). The Oslo Graben (Permian) and the Benue Trough have no province in the map.

## What I would not write

- **A single "the ocean opened at X" date for the Central Atlantic, the Red Sea, the Labrador Sea or the Southern Ocean.** For each, the abstracts I read disagree or say outright that the early history is unsettled.
- **That visitors in western Norway, Shetland, Brittany or most of the east Australian coast stand on rift rocks.** The rift basins there are offshore.
- **That the Iapetus or Rheic oceans "opened here" for places in Europe.** The European pieces of those margins have been moved and stacked by later mountain building.
- **That the East African Rift "will become an ocean".** No source read here says so.
- **Any cause.** Plumes, slab pull, squeezing-out by India: the causes of the Midcontinent Rift, the South China Sea, the European rifts and the East African Rift are all disputed in the abstracts read. The visitor sentences describe what happened and when, not why, with the one exception of the western Mediterranean, where both sources read agree on slab retreat.
- **Dates for entry 5.** They are from memory and must be checked.
